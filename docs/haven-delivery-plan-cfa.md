## How to read this plan

The proposal set out five workstreams and a phase-level timeline. This document breaks that timeline into **27 dated milestones**, each with an explicit completion test. The test matters more than the milestone. "Culture established" is not a milestone anyone can sign off; "spot audits find shift-checklist compliance above 90 percent for four consecutive weeks" is.

Three rules govern the plan:

1. **Every milestone has a completion test.** If it cannot be evidenced, it is not complete. Progress is reported against the test, not against effort.
2. **Weeks are counted from signing**, not from calendar dates. If the signature moves, the whole plan moves with it and the dates below shift by the same offset. Indicative dates assume signing on 11 June 2026.
3. **Three gates control the sequence.** Nothing downstream of a gate starts until the gate clears.

| Gate | Week | What it controls |
|---|---|---|
| Audit reported to the board (1.8) | 4 | The detail and sequencing of workstreams 2, 3 and 4 |
| NICU activation cleared (4.1) | 14 | Every activity that grows NICU volume |
| Engagement close and handover (5.2) | 26 | The decision on the optional oversight retainer |

The NICU gate is the one to hold firm on. NICU is the highest-yield lever in the building and it is also where the resuscitation risk concentrates. Growing admissions before the safety routines are demonstrably running would repeat the mistake this engagement exists to correct.

## Workstream 1 · Diagnostic audit (weeks 1 to 4)

| # | Milestone | Week | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Engagement mobilised | 1 | Point person named, week-one data request issued and acknowledged, interview schedule locked with leadership and the nursing, pharmacy and customer-service leads, facility walkthrough complete, read access granted to financial, clinical, HR and SOP records |

| 1.4 | Clinical governance and safety diagnostic complete | 2 | May mortality reviewed to root cause, emergency readiness assessed against an agreed standard, protocols and NICU readiness reviewed, staff safety-culture survey fielded and analysed |
| 1.5 | Culture, routines and incentives diagnostic complete | 3 | Frontline interviews complete, safety-culture and patient-experience results analysed, draft commission structure and JDS/KPIs assessed against what they actually reward |
| 1.6 | Operations and SOP walkthrough complete | 3 | Patient flow mapped booking to discharge, gap register naming every material SOP-versus-practice difference with an owner against each |

| # | Milestone | Week | Done when |

## Workstream 3 · Process reengineering and operations (weeks 6 to 18)

| # | Milestone | Week | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Vendor-managed inventory agreed | 10 | Terms agreed with Medbury Pharma or a benchmarked alternative, critical-items list with min/max levels signed off, replenishment and stockout escalation documented, commercial case showing the margin and working-capital effect |
| 3.2 | Management reporting layer live and reconciling | 12 | A single fortnightly pack produced by Haven, not by CFA; visit-type counts reconcile to total encounters; the receivables table foots; pharmacy reported as margin, not markup; two consecutive packs pass review without correction |
| 3.3 | Receivables process institutionalised and cash recovered | 14 | Claims submitted within an agreed SLA of discharge, ageing reviewed on a fixed cadence with an escalation ladder, at least N4.2M of the opening balance collected |
| 3.4 | Vendor-managed inventory live | 15 | Replenishment running under the agreement, no critical item stocked out for 30 consecutive days |
| 3.5 | Pharmacy and procurement economics improved | 18 | True margin measured on the new basis and improved against the week-three baseline, stock days down, working capital released from stock quantified in the management pack |

Milestone 3.4 is the one that closes the failure mode behind the mortality. The stockout that killed and the stockout that erodes margin are the same broken process.

**Deliverables:** vendor-managed inventory agreement and critical items list (wk 10) · fortnightly management reporting pack (wk 12) · receivables management process (wk 14).

## The critical path

```
1.1 mobilise ─┬─ 1.2 crash cart ──────────── 2.1 shift routines ─┐
              ├─ 1.3 receivables push ────── 3.3 receivables process
              └─ 1.4-1.7 diagnostics ─ 1.8 BOARD GATE ─┬─ 2.2 → 2.3 ─┴─ 4.1 NICU GATE ─ 4.4 occupancy
                                                       ├─ 2.4 → 2.5 → 2.6
                                                       ├─ 3.1 → 3.4 → 3.5
                                                       ├─ 3.2 reporting ─ 4.2 pricing ─ 4.3 HMO yield
                                                       └─ 5.1 ops leader ─────── 5.2 CLOSE
```

Two dependencies are worth naming explicitly:

- **4.1 cannot clear before 2.1 and 2.3.** The NICU gate is held by the culture work, not by the growth work. If the culture milestones slip, NICU activation slips with them, and that is the correct outcome.
- **4.2 and 4.3 depend on 3.2.** Pricing and HMO yield decisions are only as good as the numbers behind them. Until the reporting layer reconciles, any yield calculation is an estimate dressed as a fact.

## What Haven owns

CFA builds the standard; Haven owns it afterwards. Every milestone above needs a named Haven counterpart, and by milestone 5.2 every routine has a permanent owner. The board's specific commitments are:

- A named point person for the engagement from week one (we suggest the Head of Operations, until 5.1 fills the senior role).
- Board decisions at three points: the audit gate (week 4), the commission structure (week 13), and NICU activation (week 14). Each is on the critical path, so a delayed decision delays delivery.
- Data access as set out in the audit scope, and leadership time for interviews and the fortnightly review.

## How this tracks against billing

The payment schedule is unchanged: N2,100,000 mobilisation on signing, then five monthly installments of N1,620,000. It is deliberately not tied to individual milestones, so no milestone is under pressure to be declared complete for billing reasons. Progress is reported against the completion tests above at every fortnightly review, and against the audit baselines at week 26.
