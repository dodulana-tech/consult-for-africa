## The one idea worth leading with

> Aman does not have a 300-provider problem. It has a coverage-and-conversion problem. Solve those, and the number takes care of itself, with far less spend and far better retention.

"Onboard 300 providers" is a volume target, and email blasts and roaming field agents are built for volume. But volume is not the constraint. The constraint is signing the *right* providers, where your members actually are, using the *right* reason for a provider to say yes. Both of those, it turns out, are things Aman already has the raw material for and is not yet using.

---

## What we see in your current approach

The brief is honest about conversion being low across email, callbacks, and field agents. We would respectfully add *why*, because the reasons point directly at the fix.

1. **It is national when the problem is local.** A nationwide email to 3,000 providers spreads effort across the whole country to solve a Lagos coverage gap. Many of those providers sit nowhere near an Aman enrollee, so even a "yes" adds cost without adding coverage.

2. **It leads with awareness when the barrier is trust and economics.** Lagos providers are not short of HMO invitations. They decline because of low tariffs, delayed payment, and, above all, denied and reduced claims. An email cannot move any of those. It does not carry the one thing that would: your *zero rejected eligible claims* commitment, delivered by someone the provider trusts.

3. **It does not use Aman's two real advantages.** Your existing client book and your Islamic-affinity network are assets a generic HMO does not have. Neither is expressed in a mass email or a cold visit.

None of this is a criticism of effort. It is a case for pointing the same energy at a much smaller, much better target.

## A sharper approach, in three moves

We would rebuild the effort around three moves. This is the shape of it; the engine that produces it is the engagement.

**1. Grow the network out of your own data, not a provider list.**
Your initial Lagos members will largely be existing accounts with Lagos branches. That means you already know where demand is. We geocode your enrollees and, critically, every Lagos office of your corporate accounts, overlay network-adequacy standards, and find the exact gaps. The provider target list is then *generated* from that map, ranked, and named, so every approach can open with a fact the provider cannot ignore: "a specific, countable number of Aman members are next to you today." Because Aman is technology-first, this plays to your infrastructure rather than against it.

**2. Lead distribution through your affinity network, not cold outreach.**
As a Takaful HMO with strong links to Islamic-led organisations, Aman has warm routes that email has no way into: large Lagos-rooted Muslim organisations, Islamic-finance employers, faith-aligned hospitals and clinics, and Muslim medical networks. In this market, trust is carried by warm, faith-fluent introductions. A provider or an employer who will ignore an email will take a meeting arranged through a shared institution. This is your most defensible edge, and it is currently unused.

**3. Recruit providers with the promise you already make.**
Turn *zero rejected eligible claims* and prompt, predictable payment into a provider-facing proposition, backed operationally so it is credible. This attacks the number-one reason providers decline HMOs. Paired with guaranteed nearby volume from move one, it is a far stronger opening than any benefits-package pitch.

**A note on your own clinics.** Aman Family Clinics are part of the model, so the network is not a buy-everything exercise. As your own primary-care clinics come on stream, the external network should complement them rather than duplicate them: we prioritise the higher tiers your clinics will not cover, secondary, inpatient, and specialist, and the areas a clinic will not reach soon. Sequencing the provider map against your clinic rollout during mobilisation removes a large share of low-value signatures from the target and focuses spend where it changes coverage.

## A richer network, seeded by clinicians

The fastest way to build coverage, and the one thing no other partner can bring you, is to engage the clinicians directly. Consult for Africa operates CadreHealth, a workforce platform with more than 10,000 clinicians. From it we can curate an initial cohort of around 500 Lagos-based specialists for direct partnership with Aman, and start there.

**Why lead with the specialists**

- **Warm and fast.** These are clinicians already in our network, so onboarding is a curated introduction, not a cold canvass.
- **A coverage multiplier.** Senior Lagos specialists typically practise across several hospitals, so each relationship extends your members' access to more than one facility at once, bridging gaps quickly.
- **The hospital still wins.** When a partnered specialist brings an Aman member into a hospital, the facility still earns on the bed, theatre, nursing, diagnostics, and pharmacy. This is added insured volume, not lost business, which makes the hospital an easier partner too.

**Then unbundle the rest, so the network is richer, not just longer**

- **Pharmacy and consumables.** Capture the last mile directly through your pharmacy vehicle, or through partnership, for margin and dependable supply.
- **Diagnostics.** Arrange through dedicated partners, such as Beacon HealthCare Diagnostics, chosen for quality, price, and reach.

The result is a network organised around what a member actually needs: a specialist to see them, a facility to be treated in, medicines, and diagnostics, each sourced where it is strongest. That is richer and more defensible than a list of contracted hospitals, and it stands up faster.

## A few things the market is telling us

To show the ground we are standing on, three findings from our market work:

- **Your category is already validated in Lagos.** A Sharia-compliant HMO launched here in late 2024. That de-risks the product and confirms the affinity thesis, and it means there is a first mover to out-execute on network quality and relationships. Speed matters.
- **Roughly 40 percent of Lagos facilities accept no insurance at all,** and participation is far higher among established, larger, secondary and tertiary private facilities. That gives a precise, high-conversion segment to target rather than the whole field.
- **Payment reliability, not persuasion, is the lever.** The Lagos evidence is unambiguous that providers accept insurance for reliable volume and predictable payment, and decline it over delays and denials. Aman's brand promise is, almost word for word, the answer to that objection.

There is more where this came from. It is the reason we are confident this converts better.
