# Solace Healthcare, Abuja: pricing ladder and pushback answers

**PRIVATE. Never send this. The client-facing document is
`solace-advisory-and-proposal-cfa.pdf`.**

Client: Dr Oby Clems-Anunwa, promoter, consultant family physician at National Hospital Abuja.
Dated 14 September 2026.

---

## 1. The position

| | Anchor (published) | Floor (walk away below) |
|---|---|---|
| Setup and delivery programme, 27 months | **NGN 360.8m** (NGN 400.9m at rate card less the 10% programme rate) | **NGN 265m** |
| Resourced days | 1,325 | ~1,000 |
| Effective day rate | NGN 272k | NGN 265k |
| Mobilisation | NGN 25m non-refundable, credited to Phase 0 | Non-negotiable |
| Management, if she takes the operate track | 9% of contribution, floor NGN 12m/month, plus 20% of earnings above a 22% margin, 7 years, indexed 12% | 6% of contribution, floor NGN 8m, plus 15% above 28%, 5 years |
| Equity, operate track only | 12% of the OpCo | nil |
| Recruitment | 22% of first year cost, floors published | 18%, floors hold |

Anchor sits 36% above the floor. That is the spread to negotiate into, and she will
negotiate: she is a clinician promoter spending her own money and she will read any
number as an opening bid whatever the build says.

The programme fee is 13.8% of the NGN 2.62bn capital spend. **Never let the conversation
go to percentage of capex.** The whole point of the day-rate build is that the fee is the
effort, not a slice of her building. If she computes the percentage herself, go straight
to days: 1,325 resourced days over 27 months is about 2.3 people full time, and the cap
plus timesheets means she pays for what is worked.

## 2. The ladder, in order

Never concede two things in one sentence. Never volunteer the next rung.

**Rung 1. Drop Phase 3 from the mandate.** NGN 360.8m to **NGN 290.8m**.
Reads: very large, a fifth off. Real cost: high. Phase 3 is the highest-leverage phase
and it is where the second site and the management conversation live.
Ask in return: first right of refusal on Phase 3 at published rates for 18 months, and
the recruitment mandate for the whole programme.

**Rung 2. Defer 30% of Phase 1 and Phase 2 fees until the licence is granted.**
No change to the total. Reads: generous, and it directly answers the cash-flow objection
she will actually have. Real cost: timing only, and it aligns us with the milestone that
matters. Ask in return: exclusivity for the programme term, and the management option
held open for 12 months after opening.

**Rung 3. Trim Phase 2 by 25%.** To **NGN 269.6m**.
Framing: she appoints her own project manager for the inpatient delivery and we supervise
rather than run it. Real cost: moderate, and it puts delivery risk on her appointee.
Ask in return: we approve the PM, and the working capital workstream stays ours in full.
That workstream is the one that protects the business.

**Rung 4. The closing token.** Twenty analyst days off Phase 0, about NGN 2.2m, to
**NGN 267.4m**. Costs almost nothing, reads as movement, use only to close.

Below NGN 265m the mandate cannot be resourced with the partner and principal loading it
needs, and a thin team on a conversion programme is how we end up owning a failure.
Walk, or restructure to Phase 0 only.

## 3. Free goodwill, and what is already spent

**Already published in the document, so do not re-spend it:** third-party consultants at
cost with no mark-up, fee capped and billed monthly against timesheets, disbursements at
cost.

**Held back, spend late:**
- Stop-and-bill-only-days-worked if the measured survey or the change of use kills the
  project in the first six weeks. The cap already implies this. Saying it explicitly
  reads enormous and costs nothing.
- No mark-up on any staff we second into the business during commissioning.
- Financial model handover and training for her finance lead included, not charged.
- A named partner guaranteed on the account for the full 27 months.

**Not free, and she may ask for it:** holding the fee flat in naira across 27 months. At
current inflation that is a real concession of perhaps 15% in value. Price it as one.

---

## 4. Never concede

- Mobilisation of NGN 25m, non-refundable.
- The measured survey and the structural verdict coming before any construction
  commitment. This protects us as much as her.
- Exclusivity for the programme term.
- First right of refusal on the management mandate and on the second site.
- Deferred fees survive termination.
- Recruitment priced separately and never bundled into the programme fee.
- One naira, one fee. No fee earning twice on the same money.
- CFA methods and tooling are licensed in, never contributed as equity.

---

## 5. The best card, and when to play it

**Subordinating the management incentive to full return of her capital.** It is not in the
client document and it must not be. Costs us timing only, reads as a very large
concession to a promoter spending her own money, and it is precisely the thing a
first-time facility owner is most anxious about.

Trade it for the seven-year management term and the 12% equity tranche. Play it only in
the operate-track conversation, never in the setup-fee conversation, and never to close a
gap that Rung 4 could close.

## 6. Pushback, and the answer

**"The fee is more than I expected."**
Go to days immediately. 1,325 days over 27 months, NGN 272k a day blended across partner
to analyst. One good hospital chief operating officer fully loaded costs more than that
and cannot do the licensing, the payor strategy or the shareholders' agreement. And she
pays only for days worked, against timesheets, capped.

**"Can I start with Phase 0 only?"**
Yes, and this is a win, not a concession. Phase 0 is designed to stand alone and the
document says so. Take it, and ask for 60 days of exclusivity on Phase 1 at published
rates. Do not discount Phase 0 to get the rest: Phase 0 is where we earn the right.

**"My colleagues will do the market study for nothing."**
Let them, and say so warmly. Then point out that the two pieces that actually decide this
are the measured survey and the structural verdict on the scanner bay, and both need
instruments and a signature on a report. Nobody does those for free.

**"I know the people at the secretariat, licensing will be quick."**
She probably does, and it will still take longer than she thinks, because the filings are
sequential and the radiation authorisation is a technical submission with shielding
calculations, not a relationship. Do not argue this one hard. Let the ninety-day plan make
the point.

**"Take equity instead of fees."**
Partly, and only on the operate track. Fees do not convert at par. Anchor 12% of the
OpCo alongside the management fee, not instead of it. Never let the setup programme be
paid in equity: it is the phase with the most execution risk and the least valuation
support.

**"A percentage of contribution is confusing. Can it be revenue?"**
It can, at 4.5% of revenue, which is worth NGN 18.1m a month at month 24 against NGN 18.9m
on contribution. Roughly equivalent today. Be aware that the revenue basis grows faster
than the contribution basis if drug prices run, so it is worth more to us over time and
costs her more. If she asks directly which is better for her, tell her the truth: the
contribution basis. We lose a little and keep the relationship, and the document already
argues the contribution basis on principle, so reversing it for our own benefit would read
badly later.

**"What about AMCE?"**
She will ask, or her lawyer will. The answer is in the document and it is the strongest
part of it: we are not competing with them. AMCE is priced against Chennai and Istanbul
and has the top of the market properly. Solace takes the people who cannot reach those
prices, which is most of the catchment, and refers complexity up. If she pushes, note
that the stress case where AMCE opens a lower tier and takes a quarter of her oncology
volume still returns NGN 304m of value. That number is the one to have ready.

Do not let anyone in the room describe Solace as a competitor to AMCE. It sets an
expectation on facilities, staffing and tariff that the business model cannot carry, and
it is how a sensible day-case plan turns into an unaffordable one.

**"How do I know the drug sourcing advantage is real?"**
It is the one assumption in the document we have deliberately not banked. The base case
holds oncology direct cost at 66 per cent, which is distributor pricing. The access
programme case at 60 per cent is shown separately and is worth NGN 188m of value and
NGN 5.3m a month at month 24. Tell her plainly that we have not spent it in the model,
and that Phase 0 verifies it. Promoters trust a case more when you show them the upside
you left out.

Watch the compliance boundary here in every conversation. Her advantage is knowledge of
the pathways and direct enrolment of her own patients. It is never access to National
Hospital's stock, and the moment anybody in the room implies otherwise, correct it in the
room. That protects her career and it protects us.

**"Is NGN 400m a month realistic, or are you flattering me?"**
Neither. The base case reaches NGN 380m at month 24 from the site alone and does not cross
NGN 400m until month 28. We said that in the letter. The model is hers to run.

## 7. What to watch for in this client

- She is a serving consultant at a federal hospital. The conflict-of-interest section
  matters to her personally and she may be quietly worried about it. Handle it as
  protection, never as a warning.
- Colleague equity expectations are almost certainly already in motion. The founding
  scheme conversation is urgent and it is the piece most likely to be resisted.
- The temptation to buy a scanner early because a good price appears is real. The
  structural verdict has to land first.
- She may be attached to the "nobody does this end to end" line, because it is the
  obvious pitch and somebody has probably already said it to her. Take it away gently
  and replace it with the affordability position, which is a better story anyway.
- She has not yet been told the working capital number by anyone. It will be the most
  surprising page in the document. Expect the next conversation to be about funding, not
  about fees.
