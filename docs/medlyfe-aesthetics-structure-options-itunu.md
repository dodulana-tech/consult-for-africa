# Medlyfe Aesthetics: three ways to structure it
**Prepared for Dr Itunu Akinware · Consult for Africa · Draft**

## What this is
Three clean ways to structure Medlyfe Aesthetics with Dr Kpaduwa, set side by side. Each is built to pass the two tests that matter most: it produces a **stable service** that runs consistently, and it promises only what it can **honestly deliver**. Capital and ownership follow from those two, they do not lead. All three are designed so that every party, Medbury, Dr Kpaduwa and Consult for Africa, can name what they gained.

## First, the thing we are building
Before the deal, the service. A clinic is only worth structuring if it holds together week to week. So the design has two layers, and we are honest about each.

- **The core layer, always on.** Toxin, standard filler, skin boosters, PRP, peels, microneedling, medical facials, aftercare. Delivered by a trained resident team, a medical officer and nurse-injectors, working to Dr Kpaduwa's signed-off protocols under a resident prescriber. This layer runs the same every week. It is the stable annuity of the business.
- **The advanced layer, scheduled.** Threads, complex full-face work, and cosmetic surgery, led by Dr Kpaduwa in planned sessions with associate-clinician cover. This layer is booked around a calendar, not promised as walk-in-anytime.

Stability comes from one principle: the standard lives in the **institution and the trained team**, carried under Dr Kpaduwa's clinical leadership, not in any single diary. That is also what turns her standard into a business rather than a personal practice, which is the whole point of building it with a partner. A clinic that says plainly "here is what runs every day, and here is what runs in scheduled sessions with cover" is a clinic that holds. One that implies the founder is everywhere at once is the fragile one.

## A realistic pilot number
The earlier working figure of about N40M under-counted the one line new clinics always under-count: marketing. A premium clinic opening cold does not fill a diary on word of mouth. Built honestly, with launch-intensity marketing, the staffing that must be paid before revenue catches up, and a proper consumables buffer for FX and import lead times, the pilot funding sits closer to **N60M to N75M all-in**, the single biggest correction being marketing rising from about N7M to roughly N15M to N20M across the launch. These figures adapt international benchmarks to Lagos, since no reliable local data exists, so treat them as a sound planning range to tune, not gospel. The point for tonight is the shape, not the decimal: fund it as a real launch, not a soft opening.

## The three structures

### A. One institution, backed founder
One company holds everything. Dr Kpaduwa is co-founder and clinical director with **real equity earned for her name, goodwill and clinical leadership, no cash asked of her**. Her stake grows as the institution grows, a reward that builds rather than a lock that traps. Medbury funds the capital, holds control, owns the platform, brand, systems and data, and takes a preferred return that puts its money first.
- **Stability:** high. One balance sheet, one board, clear control.
- **Scope:** full two-layer menu under one roof.
- **Dr Kpaduwa wins:** ownership of an institution in her name, none of her cash at risk in this season.
- **Medbury wins:** control, platform ownership, first money back, and a co-founder who is genuinely bought in.
- **Trade-off:** simplest to agree, but clinical and business sit under one entity, which is less tidy than separating them.

### B. Practice and platform (two entities)
The structure that maximises stability, borrowed from how durable clinician partnerships are built worldwide. Two companies: a **clinical practice** led by Dr Kpaduwa, which owns the standard, the protocols, the sign-off and clinical quality; and a **platform company owned by Medbury**, which owns the brand, the systems, the data, the marketing, the premises and the trained team, and runs the business day to day. The platform earns a clear, fixed management fee, not a slice of clinical income, which keeps it clean and fair. Dr Kpaduwa holds **equity in the platform for her goodwill**, so she shares in the thing that scales.
- **Stability:** highest. Clinical quality is anchored to a named clinical lead; business continuity is owned by Medbury and survives independently. Neither side can hollow out the other.
- **Scope:** full menu, with the clearest line between what the team delivers and what the clinical lead governs.
- **Dr Kpaduwa wins:** she owns and leads the clinical practice, plus equity upside in the platform, no cash in.
- **Medbury wins:** it owns the durable platform outright, and the service continues on that platform regardless of any one clinician's calendar.
- **Trade-off:** two entities and an agreement between them, so a little more legal work to set up.

### C. Core and advanced (split by service)
Split the business along the line of what each partner most wants to own. **Medbury owns the always-on core service outright**, the bench-delivered annuity, stable and scalable. **Dr Kpaduwa owns and leads the advanced and surgical layer** as her craft, with a stake in it and a **royalty on the core menu she trains and standardises**, so she keeps earning from the part she hands to the team.
- **Stability:** high on the core, which Medbury fully owns and can run and scale without dependence; the advanced layer is honestly episodic by design.
- **Scope:** the honest two layers become two clear ownership lines.
- **Dr Kpaduwa wins:** she keeps the craft she loves, sheds the daily work she would rather not run, and still earns from it through the royalty.
- **Medbury wins:** outright ownership of the scalable, always-on line, with the episodic part sitting where episodic is normal.
- **Trade-off:** needs a clean revenue and brand boundary between the two lines.

## How they compare on what matters

| | Stability of service | Honest scope | How Dr Kpaduwa is backed | What Medbury owns | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. One institution** | High | Full menu, one roof | Equity for goodwill, no cash | Platform, control, preferred return | You want the simplest yes |
| **B. Practice + platform** | Highest | Full menu, cleanest split | Platform equity for goodwill | The whole platform, outright | Stability is the priority |
| **C. Core + advanced** | High on the core | Two clear lines | Owns surgery, royalty on core | The core service outright | You want to own the annuity cleanly |

## What I would agree tonight
The entity choice, A, B or C, can follow with the lawyers. What is worth locking before it goes quiet is the **set of principles all three share**, because those are what make it stable, honest and fair.

1. The service is built in **two honest layers**, core always-on and advanced scheduled, so we never over-promise presence.
2. **Continuity lives in the platform and the trained team**, owned by Medbury, so the service holds regardless of any single calendar.
3. **Dr Kpaduwa is a backed co-founder, not a hire:** real equity for her goodwill and leadership, no cash asked of her this season. A bought-in founder is a more committed and more stable partner than a paid one, which is why this is the stability play, not a giveaway.
4. **Medbury is protected by what it owns and controls**, the platform, the brand, the data and a preferred return, not by trying to tie down a person.

Agree those four, and the structure underneath is just paperwork we can finish calmly.
