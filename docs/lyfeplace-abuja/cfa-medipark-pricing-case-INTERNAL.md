# What CFA should charge for Lyfe Place Abuja, and why

**INTERNAL. Debo and CFA only.** The case for the price, argued from CFA's side. The client-facing proposal and the negotiation ladder are separate documents.

Rates below are my assumptions and need your correction. They are set out role by role rather than buried, because the whole case rests on them.

---

## 1. The decision

**Charge NGN 249M of setup fees, a base of 10% of campus-wide revenue, an incentive of 15% of contribution above a 20% cash-on-cash hurdle, and take 15% of the holding company.** Over five years that is worth about **NGN 967M** to CFA: NGN 749M of margin plus roughly NGN 218M of equity value.

Five things decide whether it holds:

1. **Your restructure is worth NGN 30M a year to CFA at an unchanged fee to her.** Replacing the campus director with a committee, replacing a facility commercial lead with two territory BDOs on the central function, and moving the agency retainer into reimbursed marketing takes cost to serve from NGN 135M to **NGN 105M**. That is not a saving to pass on. It is margin.
2. **Ten per cent is derivable, and the derivation is gross margin, not a benchmark band.** A 10% base produces a **38% gross margin** on the cost of running the operation. Eight gives 23%, thin for a services business; twelve gives 49% and looks it. Professional services run 35 to 50%.
3. **But at a 10% base the incentive has to come down.** Base and incentive are substitutes. A 10% base with a full 20% incentive takes **80% of the value the mandate creates**, and she will do that sum. At 15% over a 20% hurdle it is 74%, CFA still earns more than at an 8% base, and it survives scrutiny.
4. **The campus opex in the medipark model was understated by NGN 47M a year.** Costing the establishment properly found it. Medbury's take before fee falls from NGN 594M to **NGN 547M**, which is better found now than by her.
5. **The most valuable term is still not a percentage.** It is the right to re-size the team if revenue disappoints, and it is now worth more, because more of the cost base is CFA's to flex.

## 2. Mezo, resolved

Mezo has no company registration, so it runs as a CFA product and service offering. **CFA contracts directly and the mandate is delivered by the Mezo team.** No fee split, no separate vehicle, CFA keeps 100% of the NGN 967M. This supersedes the earlier business case, which named Mezo Health as manager at 6% plus 10% of gross operating profit.

Two things follow, and both are cheap now and expensive to retrofit:

- **Name Mezo in the client-facing proposal as CFA's facility operations practice.** It gives the operating capability a name, which is what makes "CFA is an operator" credible rather than assertive, and it costs nothing.
- **Put an assignment clause in the management agreement** permitting CFA to novate to a wholly owned subsidiary. When Mezo is incorporated you will want the contract to move without asking her permission.

**Two roles are worth arguing about.**

## 4. The base fee, derived from what CFA actually runs

| What is under management | NGN M |
|---|---|
| Campus-wide revenue | 1,697 |
| Operating expenditure under management | 1,150 |
| of which cost of goods | 400 |
| operating expenditure excluding goods | 750 |
| Contribution the operation produces | 547 |
| CFA cost to serve | 104.6 (9.1% of opex) |

| Base | NGN M | % of opex | % ex-goods | CFA margin | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6% | 101.8 | 8.9% | 13.6% | (2.8) | (3%) |
| 8% | 135.8 | 11.8% | 18.1% | 31.2 | 23% |
| **10%** | **169.7** | **14.8%** | **22.6%** | **65.1** | **38%** |
| 12% | 203.6 | 17.7% | 27.2% | 99.0 | 49% |

**The last column is the derivation.** Ten per cent gives a 38% gross margin on the cost of running the operation, inside the 35 to 50% professional services norm. It is derived from CFA's own cost rather than borrowed from a comparator band, which makes it impossible to dismiss as a made-up percentage.

**On the opex ratio, a warning.** 10% of revenue is 14.8% of full opex under management, defensible because CFA negotiates the reagent placements, consignment terms and equipment leases. Strip cost of goods out and the same fee is 22.6% of what remains, which is harder to hold. **Percentage of revenue and gross margin are the tests that survive scrutiny. Use the opex ratio internally and do not lead with it in front of her.**

### Why the incentive must come down when the base goes up

| Structure | CFA/yr | % rev | CFA margin | Medbury | CFA share of value created |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8% base, 20% over a 15% hurdle | 208.3 | 12.3% | 103.7 | 338.7 | 71% |
| 10% base, 20% over a 15% hurdle | 235.5 | 13.9% | 130.9 | 311.5 | **80%** |
| **10% base, 15% over a 20% hurdle** | **216.6** | **12.8%** | **112.0** | **330.4** | **74%** |
| 10% base, 10% over a 20% hurdle | 201.0 | 11.8% | 96.4 | 346.0 | 68% |
| 12% base, flat, no incentive | 203.6 | 12.0% | 99.0 | 343.4 | 69% |

The mandate creates NGN 294M a year over the session-let alternative. A 10% base with a full 20% incentive takes 80% of it and she will run that arithmetic. **Ten plus fifteen over a twenty per cent hurdle takes 74%, still earns CFA more than an 8% base with a richer incentive, and survives the conversation.** Note the last row: a flat 12% with no incentive earns almost what 8%-plus-incentive earns, with none of the hurdle risk. Keep it in your pocket.

Before your restructure, 8% was the fee that merely covered a dedicated team, and every naira of CFA's profit was the incentive. **After it, the base carries NGN 31M of margin of its own.** That changes the character of the deal: CFA is no longer betting the entire mandate on the incentive clearing a hurdle.

The temptation will be to price at 6.2% because you can now afford to. Resist it. She is not buying CFA's headcount, she is buying a campus that returns her capital in year two. **An operator who prices at cost-plus has not stopped being a consultant, it has just changed what it bills for.**

There is also a defensive reason. If the base is struck at cost, then every efficiency CFA finds over seven years is an argument she can make for a fee reduction at review. If the base is struck at 8% and justified on outcome, it is not.

## 5. The campus opex correction

Costing the establishment properly found that the medipark model under-provided for on-site staff and services.

| | Modelled | Costed | Short |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-site payroll | 45.0 | 75.8 | **30.8** |
| Security, cleaning, waste, insurance, internet | 15.0 | 31.0 | **16.0** |
| | | | **46.8** |

The old figure assumed a normal clinic. This runs **07:00 to 21:00, six days, across two floors and six businesses**, which needs three receptionists rather than one and a real nursing pool.

Consequences, all of which should be absorbed now rather than discovered later:

- To Medbury before fee: **594 to 547**
- Value CFA creates: **321 to 274**
- CFA's incentive at stabilisation, at 15% over a 20% hurdle: **56 to 47**

So the honest net of today's two changes is: the restructure saves CFA NGN 30M of cost, the correction costs CFA NGN 9M of incentive. **Net +21M a year, and a model that will survive her finance director.**

## 7. What the 15% actually attaches to

This is unresolved in the proposal and it is worth four times more one way than the other.

| The 15% is of | It earns | 15% at 3.3x |
|---|---|---|
| The campus services company only | ~87 | **~43** |
| The holding company consolidating all six businesses | ~339 | **~168** |

Same words, NGN 125M of difference. **Ask for the holding company and expect to be pushed to something in between**, most likely the campus company plus a carried interest in the clinical entities CFA establishes. Whatever is agreed, it must be defined in the term sheet and not left to the shareholders' agreement, because by then the answer is hers.

## 8. The downside, and the term that protects it

Campus at 60% of plan: revenue about NGN 1,018M, contribution to Medbury about NGN 121M before fee. The incentive is nil, because the hurdle is not cleared.

| | CFA revenue | CFA cost | CFA margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old cost base, 134.8 | 119.7 | 134.8 | **(15.1)** |
| Restructured, 104.6 | 119.7 | 104.6 | **+15.1** |
| Restructured, with the team re-sized | 119.7 | 68.6 | **+51.1** |

**Your restructure turns a loss into a profit in the bad case.** That is worth more than the NGN 30M of base-case margin, because it is what stops a seven-year lock-in becoming a seven-year liability.

**Negotiate hard for the re-sizing right:** an explicit ability to reduce the on-site team against agreed service levels if campus revenue falls more than 20% below the approved annual plan. It is a clause, not a percentage, and it is worth about three points on the base fee. If she refuses it, the base goes to 10% and say why at the time.

## 10. Opportunity cost, and why this matters beyond its own P&L

The Group Strategy Office retainer is NGN 54M a year at roughly 40% margin: about NGN 108M over five years on comparable senior bandwidth. **This mandate is worth about 9.2x that.**

It also does what the retainer cannot. The retainer pays for your attention and ends when it ends. This builds an operating capability with a name, a team and a track record, which is the thing you sell again. She has a stated ambition to run a platform of five to ten hospitals, and the proposal carries a first right over further Medbury facilities.

**Which is why the price here sets five prices.** At the anchor, five sites over ten years is a business. At the floor, it is five loss-making mandates consuming every senior person CFA has. The first-right clause converts a pricing error into a structural one.

If the price cannot be got right, the better outcome is a narrower mandate: delivery and establishment only, hand over on commissioning, no management fee, no equity, no first right. Roughly NGN 200M one-off and nothing recurring. Keep it on the table, because it is what makes the recurring fee negotiable at all.

## 11. Recommendation

| | Recommended | Floor |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery fee, fixed | 90 | 78 |
| Capital efficiency share | 20% of saving, cap 50 | 15%, cap 35 |
| Establishment | 22 per entity + 6 on licence | 18, licence folded in |
| Management base | **10%**, floor NGN 10M/mo | 8%, floor NGN 9M/mo |
| Incentive | 15% above a 20% hurdle | 10% above a 20% hurdle |
| Equity | 15% of the holding company | 10%, campus company |
| Team re-sizing right | **Required** | Or a flat 12% with no incentive |
| Mobilisation | 25, non-refundable | **No floor below this** |
| **CFA margin, stabilised** | **112/yr + equity** | **~62/yr + equity** |

**The floor has moved up twice.** It was 6%, then 6.5% once the costing got honest, and it is now 8%, the point at which the management service earns a 23% gross margin. Below that CFA runs a NGN 1.7Bn operation for less than it costs a professional services firm to exist. The re-sizing fallback changed too: if she will not grant it, take a flat 12% with no incentive rather than a higher base with one, because in the downside the incentive is worth nothing anyway.
