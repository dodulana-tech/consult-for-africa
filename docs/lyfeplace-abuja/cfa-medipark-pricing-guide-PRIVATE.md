# Lyfe Place Abuja: pricing the mandate

**PRIVATE. Debo only. Do not send this to Medbury.**

The proposal in `cfa-medipark-mandate-cfa.pdf` is the **opening position**. This is the ladder down from it, the floor, and the answers to what she will say.

---

## 1. The posture

She is keen. She is not price-shopping, she is deciding whether to hand CFA the whole thing. That means the risk is not that the number is too high, it is that the number is **unstructured** and gives her nothing to push against. Every element in the proposal exists partly so it can be traded.

Three things drive her: **capital back fast, small capital numbers, aggressive growth.** Every concession should be framed as serving one of those three, never as CFA taking less because she asked.

**Never concede two things in the same sentence.** She moves fast and will bank both.

---

## 2. The anchor, the floor, and what sits between

| | Anchor (proposed) | Floor (walk-away) |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery fee | 90 fixed | 78 fixed |
| Capital efficiency share | 20% of saving, cap 50 | 15%, cap 35 |
| Establishment | 22 per entity + 6 on licence | 15 per entity, licence folded in |
| Management base | 6%, floor NGN 5M/mo | 4%, floor NGN 5M/mo |
| Management incentive | 25% above a 15% hurdle | 15% above a 25% hurdle |
| Equity | 15% of the OpCo | nil |
| **Recurring, stabilised** | **213 a year (12.5% of revenue)** | **135 a year (7.9%)** |
| **Setup** | **226** | **138** |

**78 a year of give on the recurring line, 88 on setup.** That is 37% of the ask, which is about right for an opening position with a warm client. If she signs anywhere above the floor, CFA has done well. If she signs near the anchor, the mandate is worth roughly what the Group Strategy Office retainer is worth four times over.

The floor is not arbitrary. Below a NGN 5M monthly base CFA is subsidising her ramp, and below about 135 a year the mandate does not pay for the team it requires plus the risk of carrying four clinical P&Ls.

## 4. What she will say, and the answer

**"Your fee is bigger than the capital."**
They are different things. The capital is what the building costs. The fee is what the business is worth. And only NGN 71M of the setup fee is payable before a patient walks in, against NGN 235M if it were charged the conventional way. The efficiency share is paid out of money you budgeted and did not spend.

**"12.5% of revenue is too much."**
It is 12.5% of revenue and **nil per cent of the NGN 273M you would earn without us.** The fee only attaches to the increment. On that increment CFA takes two-thirds and you take a third, plus the whole of the base you already had, on 43% of the capital.

**"Why do you get equity as well as a fee?"**
A fee buys our attention for a year. An interest buys it for ten. It is also the same 15% you already agreed for CFA in the aesthetics joint venture, so it is not a new principle. If you would rather keep the whole company, option one: base goes to 8% and the incentive to 20%. *(Do not resist here. Trade the equity for the fee, or the fee for the equity, and let her choose the currency. Either outcome is fine.)*

**"Why should you earn before I get my money back?"**
*(This is the moment. Concede subordination here, and only here.)* You are right, and here is what we will do: the incentive accrues from day one but is not payable until your cumulative receipts equal what you put in. We are paid a base to run it and nothing more until you are whole. In exchange the term is seven years and the deferred fees survive a termination, because we cannot defer our profit into a mandate we can be removed from.

**"The base fee floor protects you and not me."**
It is set at what it costs to field the team, not above it. We will open the book on it. What it prevents is CFA quietly under-staffing your campus in the one year it needs the most management, which is the year revenue is smallest.

**"I will just do it in-house."**
NGN 137.5M a year fully loaded, six to nine months to hire it, the risk of hiring it wrong, no platform, no specialist network, no workforce pipeline, and nobody accountable for the outcome. Our team is cheaper than yours would be because it is shared across a portfolio and yours would sit on one site.

**"The establishment fee is just paperwork."**
It is four licensed, staffed, protocol-driven trading businesses. A recruiter charges 15% of one salary for one hire. Note that a fifth of it is payable only when the licence is actually granted, which is the outcome you are buying.

**"Cap your total."**
Yes. 12% of campus-wide revenue, all in. *(Never open at a cap. Never go below 12%.)*

**"You are only leaning the capital so your incentive hurdle is smaller."**
*(She may well spot this. It is true, so do not dodge it.)* Yes, a smaller capital base means a smaller hurdle. It also means a smaller number for you to earn back, which is the point. If it troubles you, we will strike the hurdle on total capital including the rent you have already paid. *(Ladder item 3. Costs about 4 a year. Concede it warmly.)*

## 5. Never concede

- **The capital efficiency share as a principle.** It is the strongest alignment story CFA has, it is the thing she will most like, and it is the reason a fixed delivery fee is defensible. Negotiate the percentage and the cap, never the mechanism.
- **Exclusivity**, and the **first right on further Medbury facilities.** The second site is where this mandate actually pays, because the platform is already built.
- **Deferred fees surviving termination.** Without it the whole defer-and-subordinate structure is a gift.
- **The platforms sitting outside the fee.** They are a separate revenue line and a separate negotiation. If she asks for them bundled, that is a yes-and: happy to, here is the addendum.
- **One naira, one fee.** Not a concession, it is discipline. Say it early so it reads as rigour rather than as something extracted from you.

## 6. The lean capital trade, and the trap in it

Leaning costs **NGN 51.5M a year of margin** and saves **NGN 449M of capital**. Nearly nine years of that margin to buy back what is not being spent. For a principal who wants money back fast it is obviously the right trade, and she will take it.

**The trap:** leaning is a real reduction in annual contribution, and if the campus performs brilliantly she will eventually notice she is paying lease and reagent premiums on assets she could have owned. Get ahead of it now. Build **buy-out options into every lease and placement at the outset**, priced at signature, exercisable from year two out of trading. Then the lean structure is a financing choice she can reverse, not a permanent tax. Say this in the meeting; it is the difference between clever and merely cheap.

**The other trap:** the fixed delivery fee is struck against a benchmarked budget. If the quantity surveyor comes in low, the efficiency share evaporates. Make sure the budget is benchmarked on a **conventional build specification**, which is what a QS will naturally price, not on the lean structure CFA is proposing. That is legitimate: the saving is real precisely because it comes from financing design, not from cutting specification.

---

## 7. Where to land

Target: **base 5 to 6%, incentive 20 to 25% subordinated, 10% equity, delivery 85, efficiency share 20% capped 50, establishment 18 to 20 per entity.** That is roughly **175 to 190 a year** recurring plus **200 setup**, comfortably above the floor and about 20% below the anchor. She will feel she negotiated, because she will have.

If she pushes past the floor, the honest position is that CFA cannot carry four clinical P&Ls and a build for less, and the fallback is a narrower mandate: delivery and establishment only, hand the campus over on commissioning, no management fee and no equity. Roughly 200 one-off and nothing recurring. **That is a real option and worth naming late, because it makes clear the recurring fee is buying something and not just attached to something.**
