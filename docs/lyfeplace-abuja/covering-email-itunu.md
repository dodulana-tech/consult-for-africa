# Covering email to Itunu

**Subject:** Lyfe Place Abuja: the plan, the numbers and our fees

---

Itunu,

Five documents attached. Between them they answer everything you asked for, and I have kept our fees in a separate document so you can look at them on their own.

**Where it lands.** The campus opens in month 3 on a two month fit-out. You need NGN 523 M to open it and run it for six months, and the deepest point is NGN 659 M in month 15. At run rate it produces NGN 1,598 M of revenue and NGN 400 M attributable to Medbury. That is a 41 per cent return, with your capital home in month 33.

**What the business actually is.** Downstairs is rent. The conversion clinic is a tenant, so its revenue is not ours and does not appear anywhere in these numbers. Upstairs is where you own the business, and it is a sessional one: six rooms carrying our own clinics in the working day and visiting consultants in the evenings and on Saturdays.

**So the number this runs on is consultants signed, not square metres let.** 45 of them, against a prime time ceiling of 50. The pricing behind that is not invented; it comes from the consultant survey we ran in August.

**The business plan is mostly about how those 45 get signed.** The headline is that the number is smaller than it sounds: we have to reach 417 named consultants over 18 months, which is about two a week. It also sets out a separate route to market for each of the four clinical companies.

**The one thing I would ask you to read twice** is the stress test in the plan and section 05 of the feasibility study. You can overrun the building by a wide margin and still make money. You can only miss the consultant target by about a third. This is a demand risk, not a construction risk, and the thinnest assumption in it rests on 20 survey responses.

**Three decisions need you this week**, and they are the ones that hold the month 3 opening:

1. Approve the design sprint and place the long lead orders. Cooling, medical gas and nurse call run to 12 weeks. Ordered late, nothing recovers the date.
2. Start the FCT licence application now, alongside the design rather than at practical completion. It is the only item on the critical path that money cannot accelerate later.
3. Settle the conversion clinic lease with a break at year three.

On the clinic, one thing worth flagging before you speak to Alameda. At the rate we were carrying, the campus would have been subsidising them. It costs NGN 332,214 a square metre to house them once you count rent, their share of the fit-out and the services they consume. We have quoted 400,000, which is NGN 45.2 M a year, and set it out as three lines so it can be defended component by component.

I am happy to take you through any of it whenever suits.

Debo
