# Dr Bola Akinola: The Blue Ocean Plan

**How to make the competition irrelevant, not merely beat it**

Prepared by Consult for Africa (C4A)
For: Dr Bolarinwa Akinola, FRCS (Tr. & Orth.)
Date: 23 July 2026
Status: Standalone strategy for discussion

---

## The idea in one line

Do not try to out-Oni Oni. Change the game so that the terms he competes on stop being the terms that matter.

The premium Lagos arthroplasty market is a small, crowded pool where everyone competes on the same few things: Western pedigree, a big procedure number, speed of recovery, robots, and a nice building. Dr Julius Oni currently wins that contest, and fighting him on it means spending more than he does on the things he already owns. That is a losing game.

Blue Ocean Strategy says: stop fighting for a bigger share of existing demand, and instead create new demand in space no one is contesting. The way you do that is **value innovation**, raising the value patients actually receive while lowering the cost of delivering it, at the same time. When you do this well, the competition becomes irrelevant, because you are no longer selling the same thing.

This document builds that plan. It maps the water everyone is fighting in, shows the large unmet needs no competitor serves, and lays out the moves that put Dr Bola in open water. Oni appears here as the clearest example of the old logic, not as the finish line.

**A note on evidence.** The patient-value findings below are from peer-reviewed US and Indian studies; there is no Nigeria-specific data, so we apply them by inference and flag where local validation is needed. The competitor claims are Oni's own marketing, described as such. The strategy frameworks are the Kim and Mauborgne canon (Strategy Canvas, ERRC Grid, Buyer Utility Map, Three Tiers of Noncustomers).

## 1. The red ocean: what everyone is fighting over

Draw the market as a Strategy Canvas. Along the bottom are the factors the industry competes on. Up the side is how much of each a patient gets. Today, every serious player traces almost the same curve, which is the definition of a red ocean.

**The factors everyone competes on, and where Oni sits:**

| Factor of competition | The industry norm | Oni's position |
|---|---|---|
| Western pedigree | High, and rising | Very high (Johns Hopkins) |
| Procedure volume claim | High | Very high (4,000+, self-reported) |
| Speed of recovery | High | Very high ("back on your feet in 24 hours") |
| Technology / robotics | Rising | High |
| Facility luxury | High | High |
| Price (headline) | High, opaque | High, opaque |
| Breadth of orthopaedic services | Broad | Broad (lists complex and revision too) |

Two things matter here. First, **Oni already lists complex and revision surgery**, so Dr Bola cannot claim that category is empty. He can only win it by *proving* depth that Oni asserts on paper: real revision and infection volumes, real outcomes. Second, and more important, **notice what is missing from the entire canvas**: transparency, honesty, communication, recovery support, and access. Nobody competes on these. That absence is the opportunity.

---

## 2. The discovery: what patients value is not what the industry sells

This is the crux. The evidence on how patients actually choose a joint-replacement surgeon points almost entirely at things the Lagos market does not compete on.

- **Manner and communication is the single highest-rated factor** (4.7 out of 5), just above the surgeon's demonstrated quality (4.6). Cost and convenience rate lowest (3.5). Patients choose the surgeon who listens and is trusted, not the fanciest building.
- **Patients overwhelmingly want transparency, and cannot get it.** 96 percent believe surgeons' outcomes and complication rates should be public, 95 percent their years of experience, 92 percent their case volume, and 80 to 95 percent want price transparency. Yet only about 46 percent can find any useful data to compare surgeons. This is the largest unmet need in the market, and no Nigerian competitor serves it.
- **Fear drives hesitation:** fear of a poor outcome or needing revision, and fear of facing recovery alone ("I would have to fight it all on my own"). Remove those fears and you convert people who are currently doing nothing.
- **Word of mouth and the surgeon's personal brand dominate** the decision (family recommendation, surgeon reputation and patient reviews far outrank hospital reputation and advertising). This validates leading with Dr Bola the person, and building a referral and testimonial engine rather than an ad budget.

Read together, the market sells prestige, speed and robots. Patients want to be **heard, told the truth, shown the evidence, supported through recovery, and able to afford it.** The gap between those two lists is the blue ocean.

## 3. The ERRC grid: how Dr Bola breaks away

The Four Actions Framework. To create a new value curve without simply spending more, decide what to eliminate, reduce, raise and create.

| ELIMINATE | RAISE |
|---|---|
| The pedigree arms race as the lead message (competing on whose foreign training is shinier). Let it be a quiet credential, not the headline. | **Radical transparency:** published real outcomes, actual case volumes, complication and revision rates. |
| Opaque, "call for pricing" cost. Remove the uncertainty entirely. | **Communication and dignity:** unhurried consultations, honest counsel, the patient genuinely heard. The highest-rated value driver. |
| Hype language and superlatives that every competitor already uses. | **Demonstrated depth in the hard cases:** revision, infection, salvage, evidenced not asserted. This is how he wins the category Oni only lists. |
| **REDUCE** | **CREATE** |
| Spend on facility luxury and robotics-as-marketing, which patients rate low and which drain capital better spent on outcomes and access. | **Fixed, all-in, transparent package pricing** for a joint replacement. Nobody in Lagos offers it. |
| Reliance on paid advertising, which patients trust least. | **A supported-recovery guarantee:** structured aftercare, a recovery navigator, revision reassurance. Removes the fear that stops people. |
| The transactional, discharge-at-the-theatre-door model. | **Access and financing:** instalment plans and diaspora payment, so cost stops being the barrier. And a "before you fly" second-opinion service. |

The point of the grid: every "raise" and "create" item is something patients demonstrably want and no competitor provides, and several of the "eliminate/reduce" items *lower* Dr Bola's cost base at the same time. That is value innovation, not a spending war.

## 4. The new value curve

Dr Bola's canvas should look visibly *different* from everyone else's, not higher on the same shape. He deliberately dips low on the factors the industry over-invests in (pedigree-as-headline, facility luxury, robotics-as-marketing, advertising) and rises steeply on factors the industry ignores entirely (transparency, communication, recovery support, demonstrated complex-case depth, fixed pricing, financing). A curve that diverges like that is the visual signature of a blue ocean strategy. It also tells a coherent story: **the honest, transparent, fully-supported way to have your joint replaced, at home, by the surgeon others trust with the hard cases.**

## 5. The Buyer Utility Map: where the journey is blocked

Walk the patient journey and mark where the biggest blocks to a good experience sit, the blocks no competitor removes. Each block is an opening.

| Journey stage | The block no one removes | The blue-ocean move |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | "Is my pain even treatable? Is surgery an option here?" Most sufferers never find out. | Patient education content; honest "is it time?" screening. |
| Decision | No way to compare surgeons; no price; fear of the unknown. | Radical transparency: outcomes, volumes, fixed price, published. |
| Choosing | Distrust of local capability; default to flying abroad or doing nothing. | Demonstrated depth + "before you fly" second opinion. |
| Surgery | Fear of a bad outcome or infection. | Evidenced complex-case track record; revision reassurance. |
| Recovery | "I will have to manage this alone." A major barrier. | Supported-recovery programme with a navigator. |
| Aftercare | Discharged and forgotten; no follow-through. | Structured, long-term follow-up as standard. |
| Affordability | Cost is a wall; no financing exists for surgery. | Instalment financing, diaspora payment, transparent bundles. |

The two heaviest blocks, present at the moment of decision, are **opacity** (you cannot compare or price anything) and **fear** (of the outcome and of recovering alone). A strategy that removes those two does more to grow the practice than any amount of pedigree.

## 6. The real prize: the noncustomers

Blue Ocean's biggest lever is to stop fighting for Oni's existing patients (a small, contested pool of affluent, already-converted self-payers) and instead unlock the far larger population who are *not* getting joint replacement at all. There are three tiers.

- **Tier 1, soon-to-be noncustomers.** People with worsening arthritis, managing on painkillers and local remedies, who have not seriously considered surgery. Huge in number. **Unlock with:** education, an honest and accessible entry point ("come and find out if it is even time"), and the removal of fear.
- **Tier 2, refusing noncustomers.** People who considered local surgery and said no, out of distrust, fear or cost, and now either fly abroad or suffer on. **Unlock with:** transparency (proof it is safe here), the supported-recovery guarantee, financing, and the "before you fly" second opinion that catches them at the decision.
- **Tier 3, unexplored noncustomers.** People who have never once considered that a worn-out joint could be fixed, especially outside the affluent core. Wholly untapped. **Unlock with:** community, GP, physiotherapy, employer and faith-network outreach and screening, on the Aravind model of going to find demand rather than waiting for it.

None of these tiers is being targeted by Oni or anyone else. This is where the growth is, and it is uncontested.

## 7. The value-innovation moves, ranked

Four moves. Together they constitute the new market. Ranked by impact against feasibility for a surgeon-led practice.

**Move 1 (do first): Radical transparency.** Publish Dr Bola's real outcomes, actual case volumes (including revision and infection specifically), complication and revision rates, and a clear, fixed price. This is low cost, is wanted by ~96 percent of patients, is offered by no one, and it quietly reframes the whole category: against genuine published data, a rival's self-reported "4,000 procedures" starts to look like marketing rather than evidence. It is also how he legitimately claims the complex-case authority that a competitor only lists. **Highest impact, high feasibility.**

**Move 2: The supported-recovery guarantee.** Bundle structured aftercare, rehabilitation, a recovery navigator/companion, and clear revision-risk reassurance into every joint replacement. This removes the single biggest fear that keeps people from surgery, and it drives the word-of-mouth that actually grows the practice. **High impact, high feasibility.**

**Move 3: Fixed-price bundles plus financing.** One transparent, all-in price per joint, paired with instalment financing (partnering a lender, on the model Duchess already runs with CarePay: financing part of the bill over 3 to 12 months) and a clean diaspora payment route. Evidence elsewhere shows instalment options raise elective procedure volume by 20 to 25 percent, and fixed bundles can be *more* profitable per joint, not less, when episode costs are understood. This unlocks the priced-out and the refusing noncustomers. **High impact, medium feasibility (needs a finance partner and disciplined costing).**

**Move 4: The "before you fly" second opinion, and the salvage safety net.** A deliberately easy, low-cost second opinion for anyone weighing treatment abroad, plus positioning as the place botched-abroad cases come home to. This catches Tier 2 at the exact moment of decision and turns the outbound-tourism leak into inbound demand. **Medium impact, high feasibility.**

Sequence: Move 1 and Move 2 first (they are cheap, fast and differentiating), then Move 3 (build the finance partnership), with Move 4 running alongside from the start.

## 8. It has been done: three proofs

Focused specialists beating broad, better-funded incumbents is a well-worn path. Three models to borrow from.

- **Shouldice Hospital (hernia).** By doing one operation and nothing else, Shouldice reached a 0.8 percent recurrence rate against roughly 10 percent elsewhere, treated 300,000 patients at a 99 percent success rate, and did it at about *half* the cost, with a warm, un-clinical, patient-to-patient environment. Proof that focus simultaneously raises outcomes and lowers price. **Lesson for Bola:** narrow, deep focus on the hip and knee (and the hard cases) is a strength, not a limitation.
- **Narayana Health (India).** Founded by a UK-trained surgeon (the direct parallel), Narayana delivers open-heart surgery at under $2,000 versus $100,000-plus in the US, at comparable outcomes, by having surgeons do 400 to 600 cases a year and stripping non-surgical tasks away from them, then cross-subsidising poorer patients from those who pay for nicer rooms. **Lesson:** volume plus focus plus cross-subsidy expands access without lowering clinical quality.
- **Aravind Eye Care (India).** Aravind actively *creates* demand through community camps, telemedicine and outreach rather than waiting for patients, and cross-subsidises the poor while keeping surgical quality identical across income levels. **Lesson:** the Tier 3 noncustomer play, go and find the demand, is proven at enormous scale.

The through-line: none of these won by out-luxurising a rival. They won by focusing, being transparent about outcomes, expanding access, and creating demand. That is exactly the plan here.

## 9. The brand-revamp action plan

Bringing it together into what actually changes. This complements the positioning strategy, the website brief and the referrer-engine playbook already delivered.

**Positioning.** Dr Bola (primary brand) as *the honest, transparent, fully-supported way to have your joint replaced at home, by the surgeon others trust with the hardest cases.* Osteon (secondary brand) as the place that delivers it. The complex-case authority is the credibility anchor; radical transparency is the proof; honest counsel is the voice; supported recovery and access are the felt difference.

**Offer and packaging.**
- A single, fixed, all-in package price per joint replacement, published.
- Every package includes the supported-recovery programme as standard.
- A clearly named second-opinion service (including "before you fly").
- A defined complex-and-revision pathway for referred and salvage cases.

**Financing and access.**
- A lender partnership for instalment financing (study the Duchess CarePay structure; ~40 percent deposit, 3 to 12 month terms, third-party lender).
- A clean diaspora payment route for children-abroad paying for parents.
- Explore, later, a modest cross-subsidy so a share of full-pay volume funds access for those who cannot pay, on the Aravind and Narayana model. This is also a powerful brand and PR asset.

**Proof and digital** (detail in the website brief).
- Publish the outcomes and volumes. This is the centre of gravity of the whole digital presence.
- A referral and testimonial engine (see the referrer-engine playbook), because word of mouth and the personal brand are what patients actually act on.
- Patient-education content that serves the Tier 1 and Tier 3 noncustomers and earns search traffic on the terms Oni is weak on (revision, failed, infected, "do I need a replacement", "is it safe here", cost).

**Demand creation.**
- Screening and education through GPs, physiotherapists, employers and faith and community networks, to reach the noncustomers no one else is targeting.

**Sequencing.**
1. *Now:* radical transparency (publish outcomes and fixed pricing) and the supported-recovery guarantee. Cheap, fast, differentiating.
2. *Next:* the finance partnership and diaspora payment; the second-opinion service; the referral and content engine.
3. *Then:* community demand-creation outreach; consider the cross-subsidy access programme.

## 10. What must be validated before we commit

The strategy is sound in shape, but two honest gaps need closing with local reality before large spend:

1. **No Nigeria-specific patient-value data exists.** Every preference study behind Section 2 is US or Indian. Before betting the brand on transparency and recovery-support, validate with a small round of **Lagos patient and referrer interviews** that these drivers hold locally. The discovery questionnaire and a handful of patient conversations will do it.
2. **Dr Bola's own numbers must support the transparency move.** Radical transparency only works if the outcomes and volumes are genuinely strong. We confirm his real revision/infection volumes and results (from the discovery questionnaire) before we publish anything. Transparency is a weapon only when the truth is good, and for a surgeon of his training, it should be.

Everything else, the fixed pricing, the recovery guarantee, the financing partnership, the second-opinion service, is buildable now and differentiating from day one.
