## The thesis, in one line

Lekki is the right destination and the wrong catchment. Clearview should be importing
patients from mainland Lagos, the rest of Nigeria, West Africa and the diaspora. **The
hospital is what makes a clinic a destination**, because a travelling patient's real fear
is OHSS declaring itself days after retrieval when she has flown home. Nobody else in
Lekki can say "you will be admitted by us, and it is in the price".

## The read behind the read

He said stability and sustainability. Nine years in, a founder OB/GYN who says that is
usually saying one of three things, and they lead to different engagements.

1. **Cashflow.** The lumpy months frighten him. Engagement is conversion plus a recurring
   line underneath.
2. **Succession or step back.** He is tired of being the only reason it works. Engagement
   is systems, a second clinician, management depth.
3. **Exit or investment.** Engagement is outcome data, laboratory depth, clean
   intercompany, a defensible pathway.

Question 13.1 asks him directly. Everything downstream depends on it and the strategy note
is written to work for all three.

## What to lead with on the call

Lead with the downstream obstetrics, not the tourism. It is concrete, it is flattering to
raise, and it opens the door to the rest: he built the pregnancy, somebody else billed the
caesarean. Then widen to catchment. Then the hospital as the safety net.

**Do not open with the laboratory single point of failure.** It is real, and it is what an
investor discounts first, but it threatens his sense of the business being solid. Raise it
after he trusts the diagnosis.

**Do not oversell the diaspora.** He will have heard diaspora pitches before. The useful
distinction, which he probably has not heard, is that the United States case is price (a
saving of NGN 14 to 30m a cycle) and the United Kingdom case is not (NGN 1 to 8m, so it is
really about rationing, age limits and waiting). Marketing to them as one group is the
standard mistake and knowing that is what makes the pitch credible.

## The inversion worth defending

For Dr Bola we made the person primary and Osteon secondary, because in arthroplasty the
surgeon is the product. For Clearview the note argues the opposite: **group brand primary,
Dr Kunle secondary.** Three reasons, and he may push back on all of them.

1. The brief is that the business must work without him. Deepening his personal brand makes
   the presenting problem worse.
2. A destination promise cannot rest on one man being available the day someone lands.
3. The surname collision above.

If he resists, the fallback is what we did for Bola: "Dr Kunle Ajayi at Clearview", never
Clearview alone for the clinical authority, never his name alone for the pathway promise.

## Pricing posture

Nothing priced in either client document. Correct at this stage.

The wedge is the counting exercise. Resourced build at published rates:

| Grade | Days | Rate | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partner | 4 | 750,000 | 3,000,000 |
| Senior consultant | 12 | 300,000 | 3,600,000 |
| Analyst | 10 | 120,000 | 1,200,000 |
| **Honest build** | **26** | | **7,800,000** |

**Anchor at NGN 11m. Floor NGN 7.8m.** Do not open at the build. Do not describe it as
small, cheap or inexpensive in writing or on the call. "A short piece of work with a fixed
scope" is a different sentence and it is the one in the document.

Concession ladder, one at a time, never two in a sentence:

1. Trace six cases end to end rather than ten. Saves about 900k, reads like flexibility.
   Ask in return for introductions to two of his referring gynaecologists.
2. Analyst time passed through at cost with no mark up. Cheap, reads generous.
3. Findings session folded into the fee rather than billed separately.
4. Ten per cent programme rate if he commits to the follow on mandate at the same time.
   This one actually earns something, so trade it rather than give it.
5. **Last, and only if needed:** the diagnostic fee credited in full against the first
   invoice of the follow on mandate. Biggest reading concession on the list and it costs
   nothing if the mandate lands.

Never volunteer a cap, a discount, a success fee or equity. If he asks for a payment plan,
concede it as his win, since he sells payment plans for a living and will respect it.

## Where the follow-on mandate goes

The shared care network is the real engagement and it is a CFA-shaped piece of work:
recruiting and contracting partner gynaecologists in Ikeja, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Benin,
Enugu, then Accra and Abidjan, under a written monitoring protocol with a clean fee
structure. That is consultant recruitment, contracting and clinical governance, which is
what we do. Do not table it until the counting is done.

**Watch the referral-fee line.** Paying partner doctors for referrals rather than for
clinical work is a professional conduct problem before it is a commercial one. It has to be
structured as paid monitoring work under protocol. Flagged in section 4.2 of the note on
purpose, so that we are the ones who raised it.

## The donor gamete question

Potentially the strongest diaspora hook, because Black egg donors are severely scarce in
the UK and US with multi-year waits, and Nigeria has an available pool. It is also the
single largest ethical exposure in this field: recruitment, compensation and consent of
donors, in a country where ART regulation is thin. The note says Clearview must be visibly
the most careful operator rather than the most aggressive one. **Do not let this become the
lead of any campaign.** Ask question 11.8 before saying anything encouraging about it.
