## 01. The idea: Lagos's first Harley Street

Harley Street works because the patient trusts the street before the doctor: the address does the selling, and specialists pay to sit behind it. Lagos has no equivalent, and the top of its market pays for that gap by flying abroad. This is the Lagos equivalent, in Ikoyi: not a clinic, a medical institution with two memberships under one prestige address. Medbury owns it; C4A builds and runs it and brings the specialists. It carries the Medbury name to the top of the market, is funded by recurring memberships on both sides, and pays for itself from the first year.

## 02. The opportunity: the top of the market is flying abroad

More than three quarters of Nigerian health spending is out of pocket, and the wealthiest slice leaves the country. They fly for the address, the trust and the experience, not the procedure. The market is unfinished, not empty: Duchess sells executive checks NGN 83,000 to NGN 478,000 and a flagship three-day check at NGN 4M positioned against medical tourism; Reddington runs a premium screen of the same shape. What none has built is the address that keeps the relationship, a trusted place with a family physician who knows you and the specialist on call. The opportunity is to bring both home at once.

## 03. The moat: the part no competitor can copy

- **A supply of specialists we can call.** As President of the Doctors Foundation for Care, C4A brings diaspora specialists (MANSAG, ANPA, CANPAD) to practise directly. The founding specialists are people we already know.
- **The Medbury network underneath.** Lifecheck diagnostics, the pharmacy arm, and a tertiary use-of-facilities partnership in Ikoyi (such as Lagoon) for surgery and ICU. Full pathway, no hospital to build.
- **A two-sided pull.** Specialists come for the address and the booked patients; families come for the specialists and the family physician. Each side makes the other more valuable, and the diagnostics and pharmacy revenue flows back to Medbury. A competitor would have to assemble the address, the diaspora bench, the family practice and the network at once. We hold all four.

## 04. The architecture: two businesses under one address

| Two businesses | The address | The family-practice membership |
|---|---|---|
| Who belongs | Specialists | HNI individuals and families |
| What they pay | Membership, then per session | An annual membership |
| What they get | A prestige base, member rates, offices | A family physician, an annual check, specialists on call |
| Run by | C4A operates the address | A Medbury family-practice OpCo |

Underneath both sits the Medbury network (Lifecheck diagnostics, pharmacy, tertiary pathway). The address gives the membership its specialists; the membership gives the address its patients; Medbury owns both and captures the diagnostics, pharmacy and procedure revenue.

## 05. The address: belong by membership, pay per use

Priced the Harley Street way. Membership buys the address, the serviced facility and member rates; sessions are pay-per-use; casual use is ~2x the member rate.

| Specialist membership | Recurring | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| Affiliate (fly-in / occasional) | NGN 100,000 / mo | Address and plate, serviced facility, member rates |
| Practising (resident / regular) | NGN 300,000 / mo | The above, plus priority booking and lowest rates |
| Dedicated room (exclusive) | NGN 2,500,000 / mo | A whole consulting room, held permanently |

| Pay per use, per 6-hour session | Member | Guest / ad hoc |
|---|---|---|
| Consulting room | NGN 120,000 | NGN 220,000 |
| Procedure room | NGN 280,000 | NGN 500,000 |

Two six-hour sessions a day, roughly 8am to 8pm. The two BQ offices are let whole on a permanent lease to organisations such as the DFC. Rates validated against live Med Lyfe rooms before launch.

## 06. The membership: a family physician, an annual check, specialists on call

A Medbury operating company. Concierge family medicine (the HCA London / MDVIP US model), priced to Lagos: a dedicated family physician on a small panel (~350 members/physician), unlimited priority care, an embedded annual executive wellness screen (Reddington/Duchess-level content), and quarterbacked specialist access at the address. Sold in three tiers.

| Membership tier | Per year | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | NGN 1,500,000 | Family physician, priority care, annual screen, specialist access |
| Family (up to four) | NGN 3,500,000 | The above for the family; screens for the two principals |
| Executive / Elite | NGN 6,000,000 | Royal-level annual screen, 24/7 access, home visits, fastest referral |

The annual screen is the anchor benefit that justifies a recurring fee and drives diagnostics/pharmacy volume into Medbury. Full product design and OpCo P&L in a companion brief.

## 07. The experience

**The family.** They belong to the address; their family physician knows them and takes their calls. The annual executive check happens on-site. When they need a cardiologist, the family physician walks them to one of the address's specialists that week, not a flight to London. The relationship, and the money, stay in Lagos.

**The specialist.** A diaspora cardiologist flies in a week a month, holds clinics at a prestige address with patients already booked, a nurse and front-of-house she does not hire, and a theatre pathway at the tertiary partner. A real Lagos practice without building one.

## 09. Rollout: never an empty building

- **Before the lease.** Sign founding specialists (DFC) and anchor member families (Medbury base); confirm the tertiary pathway; let Med Lyfe set live rates.
- **Months 1 to 3.** Secure and fit out the house; open the address with names on the plates; launch the membership to Medbury's HNI base with the annual screen as the hook.
- **Months 4 to 12.** Fill the roster and membership; ramp sessions; run the annual-screen engine that feeds diagnostics.
- **Year 2 and beyond.** Scale the membership, add a second address, take the model to Abuja.
