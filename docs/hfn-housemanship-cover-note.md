## Email version

**Subject:** Draft policy brief for the Minister, housemanship placement bottleneck

Dear Njide,

Following the discussion in the group and my offer to write it up, please find attached a draft policy brief on the house officer placement bottleneck, framed around solutions rather than just the problem.

I have written it in the Federation's voice, for HFN leadership to review, adopt, and place on HFN letterhead. It is addressed to Prof Iziaq Salako, Minister of State for Health, and copied to the Coordinating Minister and the MDCN Registrar.

The argument in brief:

- Roughly 2,000 of the 6,000 doctors who qualify each year are stranded, because the centralised system holds only about 4,000. Many wait six to twelve months, and some are asked to pay for placements.
- The bribery and the friction of centralised posting are symptoms. The real problem is structural: too little accredited capacity, private hospitals in particular held back by a disproportionate accreditation bar and too few slots, against a graduate pipeline the Government is deliberately widening.
- The brief diagnoses four distinct root causes and maps a specific reform onto each, so the recommendations are comprehensive rather than piecemeal.
- MDCN has itself told the Senate the answer is to bring state and private hospitals into the system, so the direction already has official backing. What is missing is the political sponsorship to execute it.
- It closes with a clear ask of the Minister and an offer for HFN to help design and pilot the model.

Please treat it as a starting draft. Edit as you see fit. I am glad to sit on any committee or working session HFN convenes to take it forward.

With best regards,

Dr Debo Odulana
Member, Healthcare Federation of Nigeria
President, Doctors Foundation for Care
