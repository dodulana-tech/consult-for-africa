## What has changed, and what has not

**What has changed is the order, and therefore the destination.** The business we are building is a residential eldercare home, then a second, then a small group of them. The care delivered in people's houses and the consultations delivered down a video link are how we get there with our balance sheet and our reputation intact.

This is not a cosmetic reordering. It changes the critical path, the capital plan, the corporate structure, the first hire, the shape of the building we look for, and what the technology has to do. Each of those is dealt with below.

**What has not changed is most of the work.** The service design, the pricing ladder, the diaspora thesis, the waiting-list engine and the cap table all survive, and several of them get stronger:

- **The diaspora thesis gets stronger, not weaker.** A daughter in London cannot personally care for her mother in Lagos. A trusted residential home is a better answer to her problem than a visiting carer she cannot supervise, it is worth more to her, and she is the segment least troubled by the cultural objection because she is already paying someone else to provide the care. Residential first sharpens the targeting rather than complicating it.
- **The waiting list becomes more valuable, not less.** For a home care business a waiting list is a pipeline of visits. For a residential home it is the occupancy plan, and occupancy is the whole economics of a care home. Every month the list is closed is a month of occupancy we are not building.
- **The cap table stands, with one structural correction.** The 51, 34, 10 and 5 split that you settled in July is right for the operating business. It should not extend to the property. That is the correction, and it is the subject of the first decision below.

**What is now wrong in the July documents.** The rollout plan puts residential in phase three as ground to be prepared. Treat that document as correct on service design, pricing, the diaspora channel and the waiting-list strategy, and superseded by this one on sequence, phasing and the critical path. The venture note and the terms sheet for Chris stand as written.

## Three decisions we take together this month

These are the three that cannot be delegated to any one of us, and all three are due before the end of September.

### One: separate the building from the business

**The decision.** Hold the property in one company and the care operation in another, rather than in a single ilé company.

**Why this matters more than it sounds.** If one company owns both the building and the operation, then Consult for Africa's 34% is 34% of a building you paid for. That is not what we earned and it is not what you intended to give. We earned our stake by building the technology and running the operation. We should not be paid in a third of your real estate, and you should not be asked to hand it over. The moment the core became a building, the single-company cap table stopped expressing the deal the three of us actually made.

**The structure that fixes it.** A property company, held by you through your holding company, owns or leases the site. ilé, the operating company, carries the 51, 34, 10 and 5 cap table exactly as you settled it, holds the brand, the platform, the clinical licences and the staff, and pays the property company rent on arm's length terms.

[[DIAGRAM:propco-opco|The property sits with your capital. The operating business carries the agreed cap table. Rent passes between them at an arm's length rate that the home can carry at realistic occupancy.]]

**What it also buys us.**

- **Clinical liability sits in the operating company, away from the asset.** In a business caring for frail people this is not a technicality.
- **The property can be financed as property.** Real estate attracts different lenders, different tenors and different pricing from an unproven care operator. Blending them makes both harder to fund.
- **The second home is a repeat of the same pattern** rather than a renegotiation of everybody's shareholding.
- **The two things are worth different multiples.** Care operators are valued on operating earnings, property on yield. Held together, one of them is always being undervalued.

**The honest costs, which counsel must handle.** The rent is a related party transaction and has to be set at arm's length and documented, and transfer pricing scrutiny is real under the 2025 reform. Set the rent at a level the home can carry at realistic occupancy rather than at the theoretical maximum, because rent is the fixed cost that breaks care homes in a bad year. Group reorganisation relief from capital gains tax is already on the open questions list in the counsel brief and now matters more.

**Disclosure.** Consult for Africa is an interested party in this decision, as we were in July. It is in our interest to hold a clean stake in an operating company. Please put this to your own legal and tax advisers rather than taking it from me.

### Two: set the site parameters, so the model can be built

We cannot model a home we have not sized. Five parameters, one conversation, ninety minutes:

| Parameter | The choice | Why it decides the model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Metro and area | Lagos, and which corridors | Decides land cost, staff travel, the families within reach and which hospitals can receive an emergency |
| Bed count band | A small first home, or a larger one | Small fills faster and proves the model. Larger carries its overhead better but is far harder to fill and far more capital |
| Acuity ceiling | Set by Chris, below | Decides staffing ratios, building specification, insurance and price |
| Lease, convert or build | Three very different capital profiles | Conversion is usually fastest to revenue and lightest on capital. Building gives the right home but is slowest and heaviest |
| Payer mix | Private pay only, or private pay plus corporate and diaspora contracts | Decides the sales motion and how quickly occupancy ramps |

**Our recommendation is to convert rather than build.** Converting an appropriate existing building gets us to revenue far sooner, at materially lower peak funding, and lets the first home prove the model before we commit to purpose built. It constrains the clinical design, which is a genuine cost, and Chris will have to rule some buildings out. That is a trade worth making for the first home and probably not for the third. This is the kind of conversion work we have done before and we will do the building assessments ourselves.

### Three: choose the funding route

**The question.** How much of this is family capital, and what carries the rest.

We are not asking for the whole answer this month. We are asking for two numbers and one direction: the capital you are prepared to commit, the ceiling beyond which the answer is no, and whether outside money is acceptable in principle and in what form. Equity in the property company, equity in the operating company, debt against the building, and a property partner who contributes the site for a share are four very different answers with very different consequences for control.

Until we have the ceiling we can describe this business but we cannot cost it, and every other number we produce is decoration.

## The platform we are building

We start the build this week and version one is live in six weeks. This section is here so that you know what is coming, what we need from you and from Chris to hit that, and what six weeks costs in scope. There should be no argument later about what version one was supposed to do.

### What it must do

Six things, and nothing else in version one.

1. **The family record.** One record per client, which follows them from first enquiry, through home visits, into a residential admission, without ever being re-keyed. This is the single most important design decision in the system and it is why we are building rather than buying.
2. **Assessment and care plan.** Chris's assessment tool, digitised, producing a care plan and an acuity score that drives everything downstream: the staffing ratio, the price and the admission decision.
3. **Scheduling and rostering.** Visits for the home care book today, shifts and rotas for the home when it opens. One engine, two shapes.
4. **The carer application.** What the carer actually uses at the bedside. Check in, the tasks from the care plan, observations, medication administration, incident reporting, check out. It has to work with no signal and sync when the signal returns, because Nigerian mobile networks will not be reliable inside a client's house and a carer who cannot record care will stop recording care.
5. **The family dashboard.** What happened, when, and by whom, with photographs where the family has consented, and a monthly written report. For the diaspora this is not a feature of the product. It is the product.
6. **Billing and payments.** Naira collections for local families, foreign currency for diaspora subscriptions, handling both subscriptions and pay per visit.

### Built residential ready from the first line

This is the part that changes because residential is the core, and it costs us almost nothing now and a rewrite later.

- **We model the client, not the service.** A client has episodes of care. A home visit and a residential stay are both episodes against the same person, with the same clinical history, the same family and the same billing relationship.
- **Occupancy goes into the data model now, even though the screens come later.** Unit, room, bed, admission, discharge. Building the modules later is cheap. Discovering that the schema cannot express a bed is not.
- **Every home care client carries escalation signals.** Acuity trend over time, carer strain, falls, hospital admissions, whether the family decision maker lives abroad, and whether the current arrangement is holding. This is the most valuable thing the bridge businesses will produce, because it is the admissions pipeline for the home, and it only exists if we design for it from the start. In eighteen months this dataset should be able to tell us, family by family, who will need a bed and roughly when.
- **Every clinical write is audited.** Who wrote what, when, and what it said before. A home that cannot demonstrate that is not defensible to a regulator, an insurer or a family.
- **Money is held as decimals, never as floating point,** and consent, lawful basis and retention are recorded per record under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

Six weeks, with something useful live in the first one. The dates below are real dates, not estimates of effort.

### What six weeks costs, and what it does not

Compression is only honest if it names what was cut, so here is the list.

- **Virtual consultations come out of version one.** They were ten to twelve week work. Until the platform carries them, we run consultations on an ordinary video tool and write the note into the client record by hand. The families will not notice. We will.
- **Rotas come out.** Home care needs visit scheduling. Rotas are a residential need, and they ship with the residential modules when there is a building to roster.
- **Foreign currency collection starts manual.** Diaspora families are invoiced and paid in sterling or dollars outside the platform until the volume justifies automating it. The subscription itself is tracked in the platform from day one, so nothing is lost.
- **The family view is a view, not a portal.** Visits, observations, photographs where consented, and a monthly report. No messaging, no document vault, no login for extended family. Those come when families ask for them.
- **The carer application queues writes offline, and only writes.** Check in, tasks, observations, medication administration, incident, check out. Full offline reading of the whole record is a much larger piece of work and it is not what a carer at a bedside actually needs.

**What is not cut.** The residential ready data model stays exactly as described above, because it costs days now and a rewrite later. The audit trail on clinical writes stays. The data protection work stays and happens before the first family record exists, not after. None of those three are candidates for compression, and I would rather move the date than drop them.

Still out of scope entirely: clinical decision support, insurance and health plan claims, a public marketplace of carers, native mobile applications, and anything that draws attention to how clever it is. A care platform earns trust by being boring and reliable.

## What could stop us

- **The building takes longer than the money lasts.** The reason the bridge businesses have to be profitable rather than promotional. We will report their contribution margin monthly from the first month, and if they are not standing up on their own we will say so early rather than late.
- **The home opens and does not fill.** The single biggest risk in residential eldercare anywhere in the world. Our answer is the waiting list and the escalation dataset, which is why both start now and not when the building is ready.
- **The cultural objection.** Some Nigerian families will hear "care home" and hear abandonment. This is real and we should not pretend otherwise. Our answer is to lead with the diaspora, who have already accepted that someone else must provide the care, to sell safety, dignity and relief rather than convenience, and to build a place that a visiting family is proud of rather than embarrassed by. The building and the language both do that work.
- **A serious clinical incident in year one.** Would set the business back further than any commercial mistake. The acuity ceiling, the policy set, the named receiving hospital, the audit trail and proper insurance are the answer, and they are all in the October work above.
- **Regulatory ambiguity.** Residential eldercare does not sit inside a settled, dedicated licensing regime in Nigeria the way it does in the United Kingdom. That cuts both ways. It gives us room to set the standard, and it leaves us exposed to a regime arriving later. Our approach is to register under what does apply, ask the relevant authorities in writing rather than assume, and build above the standard we expect to be held to eventually. A business that is already compliant when the rules arrive is advantaged by them.
- **The three of us drifting apart on what this is.** The rhythm above, the decision log and the reserved matters list exist for that reason.
