# Havana Specialist Hospital, Governance Training

## Summary of the programme delivered

**Prepared for:** The Board of Havana Specialist Hospital Limited
**Prepared by:** Dr Debo Odulana, Independent Non-Executive Director; Consult for Africa
**Faculty:** Consult for Africa, with Barr. Gesiye Otuogha and Barr. Osahon Omoruyi
**Date:** 18 August 2026
**Classification:** Confidential

> The governance training programme is complete. This is the short account of what was delivered, who taught it, what each group can now do, and what the board is asked to do next. It is written to be read in five minutes and kept as the record.

---

## 1. What was delivered

Ten modules across five domains, taught as working sessions rather than lectures, and practised on Havana's own instruments rather than on generic examples.

| Domain | Modules | Led by |
|---|---|---|
| Legal and statutory foundations | M1 CAMA 2020 and the company as a legal person; M2 directors' duties, liability, and conduct | Barr. Gesiye Otuogha, with Barr. Osahon Omoruyi |
| The board's role and operating model | M3 governing versus managing, and the HSH operating model | Consult for Africa |
| Board and committee mechanics | M4 running compliant, effective meetings; M5 reading the board pack and asking good questions | Barr. Osahon Omoruyi with the Company Secretary; Consult for Africa |
| Assurance, risk, and the hospital dimensions | M6 clinical governance from the board's seat; M7 financial oversight, audit, and internal control | Consult for Africa with the legal faculty |
| Conduct, fairness, and leadership | M8 conflicts and fair process; M9 chairing and committee leadership; M10 the management interface and the Afya relationship | Consult for Africa, with Barr. Omoruyi |

Three cohorts were served: the full board, the committee chairs, and management with the Company Secretary. The Medical Director sat in both the board cohort and the management interface, because that dual position is the clearest worked example of governing versus managing and was taught as such.

**Company law ran through the programme as a spine, not as a single lecture.** Barr. Otuogha carried the Act on the page and Barr. Omoruyi carried the boardroom in practice, drawing on live hospital cases. Consult for Africa carried the operating model and the governance craft. Every module named the legal duty it engages, so directors' duties were reinforced ten times rather than covered once.

---

## 2. What each group can now do

- **Every director** understands their duties under CAMA, can read and challenge a board pack, handles a conflict correctly, and governs without reaching into management.
- **Every committee chair** can set an agenda with the Company Secretary, run a compliant meeting, draw out every voice, reach and record a decision, and report to the board on one page.
- **Management and the Company Secretary** can report by exception, escalate correctly and without being asked, run a compliant meeting cycle, and keep the board's statutory records to standard.

The board also now shares a common language with management about what good reporting and good challenge look like, which was the purpose of teaching some modules to both sides.

---

## 3. The instruments that came with it

Training without instruments does not change a single meeting. The **Board and Committee Instruments Pack** accompanies this summary and contains the eleven working instruments the board and committees now run on: the consolidated board pack, the agenda, the one-page board paper, the committee report used in common by all five committees and the Afya-HSH Governance Committee, the management report by exception, the performance dashboard and KPI pack, the risk register summary, the minutes, the action and decision log, the escalation thresholds schedule, and the registers of interests and attendance. A rolling twelve-month board and committee calendar sits alongside them.

Each instrument is also provided as an individual Word or Excel file, so the Company Secretary, the chairs, and the executives can use them immediately rather than rebuilding them.

---

## 4. How the programme closes

A light, non-punitive competency check per cohort, so the board can be assured the capability is real rather than assumed:

- **Directors** complete a short scenario exercise, a conflict to spot and handle, a variance to question, and a decision to frame, plus a self-assessment against the module objectives.
- **Committee chairs** are assessed by chairing a live mock committee meeting against a simple rubric: agenda, time, voices heard, conflict handled, decision reached and recorded.
- **Management and the secretariat** produce a compliant exception report and run one meeting cycle to standard.

The mock is run as the Finance, Investment and Operations Committee, deliberately, because it is the committee co-chaired across both founding families and therefore the only place where fair co-chairing and the escalation of a genuine deadlock can be practised rather than described.

The Company Secretary records completion, and the People and Remuneration Committee receives a one-page assurance that each cohort has met its objectives, with any gaps and the plan to close them. Directors are named in attendance and unnamed in scoring, because the board needs to know the capability is there and does not need a league table.

---

## 5. What the board is asked to do in September

The framework becomes real when it is adopted. Six decisions do that.

1. **Adopt the instrument suite**, instruments 1 to 11, with effect from the September meeting.
2. **Set the escalation thresholds**, filling the bracketed values in the schedule.
3. **Set the RAG thresholds** for the dashboard metrics.
4. **Adopt the calendar**, and confirm the six-weekly cadence for the first three meetings before settling to quarterly.
5. **Confirm the delegation thresholds** in the Delegation of Authority schedule, which the escalation schedule depends on.
6. **Instruct the Company Secretary** to administer the suite, maintain the registers, and report compliance with the seven-day pack rule at each meeting.

Two points also need confirming, and they sit with the Company Secretary rather than with the board: **Havana's financial year end**, which fixes the annual general meeting and the annual return deadline in the calendar, and the **confirmed date of the September board meeting**, which sets the pack circulation date seven clear days before it.

---

## 6. The test

The test of this programme is not that the board and management sat through it. The test is that the September meeting runs like the model: papers out seven days ahead and read, conflicts declared and minuted, exceptions challenged rather than received, decisions recorded with their reasons, and the Afya partnership taken as a standing item with the board's own independent line of sight intact.

That is a well-governed hospital, and Havana is now equipped to be one.
