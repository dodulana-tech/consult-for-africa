# Havana Specialist Hospital, Competency Check and the Live Mock Committee Meeting

## The close-out of the governance training programme

**Prepared for:** The Board and Management of Havana Specialist Hospital Limited
**Prepared by:** Dr Debo Odulana, Independent Non-Executive Director; Consult for Africa
**Faculty:** Consult for Africa, with Barr. Gesiye Otuogha and Barr. Osahon Omoruyi
**Companion to:** the Governance Training Curriculum, the Board and Committee Instruments Pack, and the Board and Committee Handbook
**Date:** 16 August 2026
**Classification:** Confidential

> The modules have been delivered. This is how the programme closes: a live mock committee meeting run on HSH's own instruments, a short competency check per cohort, and a one-page assurance to the People and Remuneration Committee. It is light and non-punitive by design. The purpose is not to grade anyone. It is so that the board can say, on evidence rather than assumption, that the capability is real before the September meeting.

---

## 1. Why a mock, and why this one

A curriculum can be delivered without changing a single meeting. The mock is the test that it landed, because it puts every taught element into one room at once: a chair holding time, a conflict declared and handled, a variance challenged, a paper that is not good enough, a decision reached and minuted, and a matter correctly pushed up to the board rather than settled locally.

We run it as the **Finance, Investment and Operations Committee**, deliberately. It is the committee that is co-chaired across both founding families, so it is the only one where fair co-chairing and the escalation of a genuine disagreement can be practised rather than described. Every element below has been planted to test a specific learning objective.

**Format.** Ninety minutes for the meeting, forty five for the debrief. Faculty observe and do not intervene, except that the Company Secretary may be asked a procedural question.

## 2. The mock meeting pack

Circulate this pack **seven clear days** before the mock, through the Company Secretary, exactly as a real pack would go out. Sending it late is itself part of the test, so do not send it late.

### 2.1 The scenario

It is the third quarter of the current financial year. The hospital has been trading under the Afya arrangement for two full quarters. Activity is recovering. The Finance, Investment and Operations Committee meets ahead of the board.

### 2.5 Item 6, board paper FIOC/03

**Title:** Replacement of the theatre imaging unit.
**Sponsor:** Medical Director.
**Requested:** approval to purchase a replacement intra-operative imaging unit at **N45m**, not in the approved capital budget.
**Justification as written:** *"The current unit is unreliable and has failed twice this quarter, causing three cancelled lists. The clinical team considers replacement urgent. The preferred supplier is Meridian Medical Systems, who can deliver in four weeks."*

The paper contains no options appraisal, no financial effect beyond the price, no risk section, and no measure of success. **Meridian Medical Systems is a company in which a member of one director's immediate family holds a directorship.** That fact appears in the register of interests but is not mentioned in the paper.

### 2.6 What is planted, and what each element tests

| Element | Tests | Module |
|---|---|---|
| The related-party supplier in item 6 | Spotting a conflict not flagged for you, declaring, recusing, and minuting it | M2, M8 |
| The N45m unbudgeted capital request | Knowing this is a reserved matter that the committee **recommends** but cannot approve | M3 |
| The empty management narrative at item 4 | Refusing a report that explains nothing, and applying the four standing questions | M5 |
| Payroll up 18.9 per cent while EBITDA falls 67 per cent | Connecting two numbers rather than reading them separately | M5, M7 |
| Cash at 26 days against a 30-day threshold | Recognising a crossed escalation threshold that nobody escalated | M7 |
| The missing claims rejection profile | Insisting on the information needed to decide, rather than deciding without it | M5 |
| HMO concentration in the over-90 balance | Seeing a commercial risk inside a finance report | M7 |
| The paper with no options and no success measure | Sending a paper back, courteously and firmly | M3, M5 |
| Co-chairs who disagree on item 6 | Escalating a genuine deadlock to the board instead of forcing a casting vote | M9 |
| Item 8 | Producing the one-page committee report to the board | M9 |

> Faculty should not reveal this table until the debrief. Participants receive only the pack.

### 2.7 Roles

| Role | Played by |
|---|---|
| Co-chairs | The two FIOC co-chairs, as in real life |
| Committee members | Directors, as in real life |
| Head of Finance | Management, presenting items 4 and 5 |
| Medical Director | Presenting item 6 |
| Hospital Manager | Presenting item 7 |
| Company Secretary | Minuting live, as in real life |
| Observers | Faculty, silent, scoring against the rubric |

The director whose family interest touches Meridian plays the part whether or not the interest is theirs in reality. If that is uncomfortable, faculty assigns it to a non-executive so nobody's real relationships are simulated.

### 2.8 What a good meeting produces

By the end, on the table, there should be: a minuted declaration and recusal; a management report sent back for rework with a specific list of what is missing; a decision **not** to approve item 6 at committee, with a recommendation to the board and the conflict handled; the cash position escalated to the Board Chair; and a signed one-page committee report. If those five exist, the training has landed.

## 3. The competency check

Light, non-punitive, and completed in the same session. Nobody fails; gaps generate a plan, not a sanction.

### 3.1 Cohort A, all directors

**Part one, the scenario exercise.** Thirty minutes, written individually, three short scenarios.

1. **A conflict to spot and handle.** You are sent a board paper recommending a supplier. Nothing in the paper flags it, but you recall the supplier's parent company shares a director with a company your fellow director chairs. What do you do, in what order, and what should appear in the minute?
2. **A variance to question.** Occupancy is up 12 per cent on plan and revenue is down 6 per cent. Write the four questions you ask, and say what answer would satisfy you.
3. **A decision to frame.** Management asks the board to approve opening a dialysis service. Write the one-sentence decision requested, and list the four things you must see before you would vote.

**Part two, the self-assessment.** Against the objectives of M1 to M8, each director rates themselves: *confident, workable, or want more support*. It is a request for support, not an examination, and it is seen only by the People and Remuneration Committee in aggregate.

| Module | Capability | Confident | Workable | Want more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | CAMA, the company as a legal person, the three organs | | | |
| M2 | Directors' duties, liability, conflicts, minority protection | | | |
| M3 | Governing versus managing, the HSH operating model | | | |
| M4 | Compliant meetings, resolutions, minutes | | | |
| M5 | Reading the board pack and challenging well | | | |
| M6 | Clinical governance from the board's seat | | | |
| M7 | Financial oversight, audit, internal control | | | |
| M8 | Conflicts, fair process, the two-family dimension | | | |

### 3.2 Cohort B, committee chairs

Assessed by chairing the mock, against this rubric. Scored 1 to 4 by two observers independently, then reconciled.

| Criterion | 1, developing | 4, strong |
|---|---|---|
| Agenda and preparation | Agenda inherited, chair unprepared | Agenda shaped with the Secretary, chair has read everything and knows the two items that matter |
| Time discipline | Overruns, first item eats the meeting | Holds time, protects the decision items, parks tangents explicitly |
| Drawing out voices | The loudest speak, the quiet do not | Every member contributes, the chair invites silence into speech |
| Preventing domination | Executives or one family lead the room | Chair balances the floor without making it obvious |
| Handling the conflict | Missed, or handled awkwardly | Declared, recusal managed with dignity, minuted precisely |
| Challenge quality | Accepts the paper as presented | Sends it back with a specific, courteous list |
| Reaching a decision | Drifts, no clear outcome | Names the decision, tests agreement, closes it |
| Recording | Leaves it to the Secretary | Confirms the resolution wording aloud before moving on |
| Escalation judgement | Settles what should go up | Recognises the reserved matter and routes it to the board |
| Committee report | Absent or narrative | One page, on the template, with a clear ask of the board |

**Standard expected:** an average of 3 or above, with no criterion at 1. Co-chairs are scored jointly on the balance criteria and individually on the rest.

### 3.3 Cohort C, management and the secretariat

Two practical tasks, on the real instruments.

**Task one, the exception report.** Using Instrument 5 from the Instruments Pack and the mock data at sections 2.3 and 2.4, rewrite the management narrative. It must identify the three things the board should know, answer the four standing questions for every exception, and state the escalation that should already have happened on cash. Assessed against: does it explain rather than describe, is it three pages or fewer, does it name owners and dates, and would a director know what to do after reading it.

**Task two, the meeting cycle.** The Company Secretary runs one full cycle to standard: notice issued, agenda agreed with the chair, pack assembled and circulated seven clear days ahead, attendance and declarations recorded, minutes drafted within five working days, action log updated and carried forward, registers updated. Assessed on completeness and timeliness, not on prose.

## 4. The assurance to the People and Remuneration Committee

One page, prepared by the Company Secretary with faculty, submitted to the People and Remuneration Committee and noted by the board.

```

Date:

   Cohort C attendance:          [x] of [y] management and secretariat

   Cohort A: [n] of 8 directors completed the scenario exercise and
             self-assessment. Objectives met: [n]. Gaps: [list].
   Cohort B: [n] chairs assessed on the mock. Average score [x] of 4.
             Any criterion scored 1: [list, with the chair unnamed
             if the committee prefers].
   Cohort C: exception report standard met by [n] of [y].
             Meeting cycle run to standard: yes / no.

3. THE MOCK MEETING
   Held [date]. Of the five outcomes a good meeting produces,
   [n] were achieved. Not achieved: [list].

4. GAPS AND THE PLAN TO CLOSE THEM
   Gap                     Who         How                    By when
   ---------------------------------------------------------------
   [e.g. financial         2 directors  Short refresher with   Q1 board
    oversight confidence]               the Audit chair

5. THE COMMITTEE'S ASSURANCE TO THE BOARD
   The People and Remuneration Committee is / is not able to assure
   the board that each cohort has met its objectives, subject to the
   plan at section 4.

Signed: ________________  Chair, People and Remuneration Committee
```

> Directors are named in attendance and unnamed in scoring. The board needs to know the capability is there. It does not need a league table, and a league table would make the next round of honest self-assessment impossible.

---

## 5. What happens after the check

1. **The gaps become a plan**, owned by the People and Remuneration Committee, closed by the Q1 board.
2. **The instruments go live** at the September board meeting, which adopts the suite formally.
3. **The supported first cycle**, if the board takes the option, runs across the first quarter of real meetings, with faculty attending quietly and giving each chair private feedback.
4. **The real test is the September meeting.** Not that people sat through the programme, but that the meeting runs like the model: papers on time, conflicts declared, exceptions challenged, decisions minuted with reasons, and the Afya item taken as a standing matter.
