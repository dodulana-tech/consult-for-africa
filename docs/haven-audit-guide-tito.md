## 2. Read this first — the sensitivities that govern everything

This is not a normal turnaround. Internalise these before you speak to anyone at Haven.

### The incident — how we hold it
In May 2026 Haven lost a child. The child was **referred in already critically ill and may not have been saveable under any circumstances; the loss is not attributable to the facility.** It is what prompted leadership to call us.

- **We do not raise it. Ever. In writing or in conversation.** Not in the data request, not in interviews, not in the report, not in a WhatsApp message. If a staff member raises it, listen with care, do not probe for blame, and move on.
- **Every client-facing word is forward-looking:** we are here because a strong young facility is putting world-class systems under its growth — *not* because something went wrong. That is the honest, respectful, and legally sound framing.
- **Why it's this strict:** documents travel (a proposal or report is discoverable). A written record linking a death to any facility gap is a liability for Haven and for us, and it reopens a wound for a grieving board — one of the owners is a former co-founder of mine. The point of the audit stands entirely on its own without ever invoking the loss.

### The relationship & governance
- I was **invited to Haven's board from inception but do not formally sit on it.** Say "invited to the board," never "sits on the board." The board is not yet formally constituted.
- CFA is a **paid partner** to a company I'm close to — a related-party situation we handle with total transparency: pricing disclosed to all owners, clean fixed fees, **no success fee.** You don't need to manage this, but know it, and never imply special treatment or leverage.

### The commercials
- Fee is **N9.3M** (negotiated down from a standard N17M — a large concession already made). Do not use the discount as leverage or discuss it as a favour. If payment comes up, it comes to me.

### The thesis (say this, it's ours)
This is a **culture and systems build, not a set of process patches.** Strong routines, standards of work and incentives are what make safe, efficient care self-sustaining. Everything we recommend ladders up to that. "Culture is the multiplier" is the line.

## 3. What Haven is

A ~15-month-old private facility in GRA Ikeja: **5 general-paediatric beds + a 3-bed NICU.** Owners: Mr Kabir Aregbesola, Mrs Abisodun Alli, Dr Shakirah Saliu, Dr Odedina (Medical Director), and Mr Ogochukwu Odum. It funds its own salaries and has a real local reputation — a genuine achievement for a facility this young. It is now scaling into more complex care (NICU), which is exactly when the systems beneath it have to be deliberate rather than assumed.

**The three business truths shaping our work:**
1. **The cash problem is a working-capital problem, not a profit problem.** ~N4.2M is locked in HMO receivables (Leadway + NEM ≈ a full period's revenue) and ~N2.77M in pharmacy stock — roughly **N7M tied up.** The money isn't missing; it's on the shelf and in the HMO ledgers. Freeing it is our fastest, most visible win.
2. **NICU is the growth engine** (≈N3M deposit per admission, 3 beds) — and it only scales safely once governance and standards are in place. Fix the foundation, then fill NICU. That's the through-line.
3. **The reporting layer is immature, and that itself is a finding** — visit counts don't reconcile, the receivables table doesn't foot, a "201% pharmacy profit" is a markup not a margin. You can't manage what you can't measure. A senior operations leader (which we'll help recruit via CadreHealth) is part of the answer.

## 4. The audit playbook — four weeks

Your toolkit is already built and lives in `docs/`: the **Information & Data Request** (the branded PDF goes to their point person), the **staff safety-culture survey** (online form), the **fieldwork kit** (your interview guides + observation checklist — internal), and the **crash-cart standard** (the day-one quick win).

| Week | Where | What you drive |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Kick-off + remote | Get a named point person (push for the Head of Operations). Issue the data request; flag the **12 Week-1 critical items.** Launch the staff survey. Put the crash-cart standard live. Kick off the Leadway/NEM receivables push. Start reconciling the management accounts. |
| **2** | On site | Interviews (owners, clinical lead, neonatology, matron, pharmacy, front desk, accounts) using the fieldwork guides. Observation across shifts. SOP walk-throughs. NICU readiness. |
| **3** | On site + remote | Finance & working capital; procurement & inventory (do physical spot-counts against the system); HMO economics; reporting-integrity reconciliation. Close the survey. |
| **4** | Remote + board | Synthesise into a **prioritised, costed** plan. Present the two deliverables to the board. |

**Two deliverables:** (1) Operational & Clinical Governance Audit Report; (2) Working Capital & Receivables Recovery Plan. Both are walked through in a board working session — never just emailed to file.

## 5. How we work — the posture

- **Collaborative, not audit-by-ambush.** The team is a partner in the findings, not a target. Open every interview by saying so. We are diagnosing a system, never blaming a person.
- **"Nothing here is a test."** Partial or messy records *are* a finding. Take what exists; don't make anyone reconstruct things.
- **Observe the real operating day,** not the documented one. Watch a handover, a drug round, a patient from arrival to billing.
- **Reconcile numbers against each other** — visit counts vs encounters, receivables that should foot, stock system vs a physical count. Where they disagree is where the story is.
- **Protect the survey's anonymity fiercely.** Haven is small; report only grouped totals, never anything that could identify a respondent, and never let management see raw responses. Honesty depends on it.
- **Confidentiality throughout.** One secure, read-only shared folder. Sensitive data is used only for this audit.

## 6. What to look for (your analytical priorities)

- **Free the cash:** size the receivables precisely, get the Leadway/NEM recovery moving in week 1, and quantify how much of the N2.77M stock is releasable working capital.
- **Real pharmacy margin** vs the markup illusion; where margin leaks between purchase and sale; the case for vendor-managed inventory (we lean toward recommending Medbury Pharma).
- **Reporting you can trust:** what it takes to give the board numbers that reconcile.
- **Culture & incentives as the lever:** the staff commission structure and the JDS/KPIs already awaiting board approval are the exact tools a strong culture runs on — the survey tells us where the gaps are.
- **NICU safe-scaling:** what governance, staffing and kit must be in place before admissions grow.
- **Shift-level routines:** the crash-cart check and handover discipline are the visible proof the culture is changing.
